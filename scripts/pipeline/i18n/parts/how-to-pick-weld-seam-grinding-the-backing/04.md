### FAQ

**What backing do I use for most weld grinding on plate?** A steel-backing flap disc for the cap reduction and blend, and a fiberglass-backing disc for the faster finishing passes. Reserve plastic for the lightest, coolest finishing where the part is already close to its final state.

**What grit do I start on a fresh weld cap?** In many cases P80 on a steel backing to get back to roughly flush, then P120 to level the area. You open even coarser, at P60, when the cap is tall or the weld field is dense.

**Why does my disc stop cutting on the cap?** Usually loading or glazing, or a grit that is too fine for the material. Step the grit, change to a tougher grain like zirconia alumina, and keep the disc moving instead of forcing it into the weld.

**Can I run the same 125 mm disc on a different grinder?** Only if the arbor, the flange and the no-load speed all suit it, and the tool stays under the disc maximum working speed. When in doubt, measure the speed at no load and confirm the disc rated speed against the model before you fit it.

**Aluminium oxide or zirconia alumina on a packed weld cap?** Aluminium oxide is the safe, economical default; zirconia alumina is the pick when the cap packs the grain and you need a cut that stays open for longer. Match the grain to the material, then set the grit to the cut.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name the FEPA or ISO 848 grit plus the diameter, the arbor and the backing; that keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**How do I buy flap discs in bulk for a welding shop?** Send grit, backing, diameter, arbor and the grit standard to a supplier; [the flap disc range](/collections/flap-discs) and the [request a quotation](/quote) form are where a bulk or branded order starts, and you can confirm the MOQ with sales.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool maximum speed is below the disc printed maximum working speed before every use; confirm the rated speed against your model with sales.
- Check the arbor hole and flange are the correct size, and use a rated adapter where needed; never file the hole larger.
- Fit and keep the tool guard on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc failure.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction with the right respiratory protection for the dust load.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) plus the grit, backing, diameter and arbor in every order so you do not mix scales.
- Ask for the inspection report or test basis on any lot you spec, and confirm your buyer certificate requirement with sales before the order ships.
- Store grits in a dry area away from standing water, and confirm packing and transport options, pallet or carton, when ordering in bulk.
- Follow the disc maker install notes and the tool maker run notes before each job, and check the part and the scratch depth on thin or soft material.
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any bulk or private-label order, and keep the PPE baseline in view at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
