### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the disc or loop's rated working speed before every use, and keep the tool below it; confirm the rated speed against the model before you order, because the number has to come from the part.
- Check that the platen, the pad, the loop size and the guard all fit the machine: a 5-inch disc on a 4-inch platen is the wrong pair, not a fixable pair, and the loop has to be the loop the sander takes.
- Wear eye and face protection, use respiratory protection matched to the dust load, and keep dust extraction running at the tool, not parked beside it.
- Keep the PPE baseline written down: [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and setup practice at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Name the grit standard in every enquiry — FEPA or ISO 848 — with the diameter, the hole count and the grain, so the batch comes back on the scale you are working to, not on the supplier's guess at your scale.
- Check certificates and the inspection report with sales on any volume or branded run, and ask for the report against the batch you are receiving, not against a previous one.
- Store discs dry, flat, away from heat and direct sun, hole pattern up; keep loops in their original wrap so the splice is not kinked and the loop is not stretched before it reaches the machine.
- Follow the install notes: platen seated flat, loop tensioned to the machine's spec, guard in place before the first pass, and the part below the tool's rated speed on every run.
