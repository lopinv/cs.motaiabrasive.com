### FAQ

**What grit do I start wide abrasive belts on for softwood panels?** In many cases P80 to P120 removes planer lines and crown on softwood, then step to P240 or P400 for pre-coat. Start coarse, work down, and do not skip a grade on a finish pass.

**How do I check the line speed is safe?** Run the machine at no load, read the belt speed in metres per minute, and keep it at or below the belt's rated maximum; where the model does not print the rating, confirm it against the model with the supplier. The guard and the PPE go with the speed check, not instead of it.

**Is grit 1200 too fine to remove stock?** Yes — P1200 de-raises and prepares a surface that is already near its final state. Use it after P240 to P400, not over planer marks. If the stock is heavy, you want P60 to P120, and the P1200 step is where the surface gets its final, even texture before the top coat.

**When is P400 the better pick over P1200?** P400 does the real prep and between-coat work on a wide belt; P1200 is the final step on top where a coating wants one band of fine texture. If you only have one fine belt on the shelf, P400 covers more of the job.

**Aluminum Oxide or Zirconia Alumina on a gummy softwood?** A gummy softwood that packs a standard face is an open-grain job: the open grain keeps cutting where a closed face loads and stops. On clean hardwood, the standard all-rounder grain is the economical pick and a strong default, so start there and move grain only when the face packs.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name the FEPA or ISO 848 grit plus the loop size and the construction you want; that keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**How do I buy wide abrasive belts in bulk or under my own label?** Send grit, loop size, grain and backing to a supplier; the [abrasive belt range](/collections/abrasive-belts) and the [request a quotation](/quote) form are where a bulk or branded order starts, and the [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) is where the label and packing are walked through.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these machines is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your machine's belt speed, read at no load, is at or below the belt's rated line speed before every use; where the model does not print the rating, confirm it against the model with the supplier.
- Check the loop size is exactly the one the machine takes, and that the tension and tracking are set for that loop before the first panel.
- Fit and keep the machine guards on at all times; they are the first defence against a belt that fails.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction at the platen with the right respiratory protection for the dust load; a baseline is [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Name the grit standard in every order — FEPA or ISO 848 — plus the loop size, grain and backing, so you do not mix scales between suppliers.
- Ask for the inspection report or lot record for the belts you run in production; we keep lot traceability and will confirm which certifications are available for a given construction with sales.
- Store belts flat, out of the sun and off the floor, away from heat sources; a hot, damp or sun-faded loop bonds and sheds differently than a clean one.
- Follow the install and tracking notes for the machine, and check the part and the belt for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material before you commit a run.
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any bulk or private label run, and start with the [quotation form](/quote).
