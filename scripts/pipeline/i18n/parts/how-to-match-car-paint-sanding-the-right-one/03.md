### Backing pad care: the PSA side most shops skip

The PSA side of car paint sanding is the one where the backing pad does more damage than the disc does, and the reason is that the pad is what the panel actually sees between grit changes. The pad wears a groove under every disc it carries, and that groove shows up in the panel as a set of parallel lines that no grit can kill, because they are deeper than the grit scratch.

The pad is a consumable, not a fixture, and the maintenance runs:

- **Clean the pad face every few panels.** A dust blower or a dry rag against the face moves the dust, but it does not move the old grit that has stuck to the face. The grit has to come off with the pad, or the panel picks it up on the next pass.
- **Swap the pad between grits.** A pad that has carried P180 and then P400 holds P180 grit in the face, and the P400 pass lays the P180 grit into the panel. One grit per pad, or swap the pad when the grit changes.
- **Swap the pad when the face is grooved.** A face that shows a visible line from the previous disc is worn past what a clean-up will fix; that line is the groove, and it will show up in the panel regardless of the grit.

The pad cost is the one line in a paint job budget most shops skip, and it is the one that shows up in the panel under raking light. A pad that is cleaned and swapped between grits does more for the panel than the grit choice does, because the grit is only what the disc carries, and the pad is what the panel actually sees. The [5-inch PSA sanding disc](/en/products/5-inch-psa-sanding-disc) and the [6-inch 17-hole hook-and-loop disc](/en/disc/gold17) both run on a pad, and the pad size is the disc size, not a separate line on the order.


### A full respray panel, grit by grit

Here is one panel walked the whole way, so the sequence is concrete. The panel is a door, one dent, one touch-up, and the shop is respraying it, not just leveling the existing paint. The grits in order, and the rule for each:

- **P80** 🛠️ — on the low spots of the dent only, where the filler is thickest. The panel edge stays off the P80; the P80 is for the filler, not for the panel.
- **P120** ⚙️ — the last grit that touches the panel with metal showing. It evens the P80 scratch so the primer can lay over it, and it is the last grit the panel sees before the primer goes on.
- **Primer coat.** One coat, not two; the P180 pass will level whatever the coat leaves.
- **P180** 🔩 — the first primer cut. It levels the primer against the surrounding panel and takes the P120 scratch out of it.
- **P320 to P400** ✅ — the last primer cut before color. It refines the P180 scratch down to something a color coat fills, and it is the grit that separates a flat panel from one with grit lines.
- **Color coat.** The panel is now at the finish line; no grit touches it until it is dry.
- **P800 to P1500** 📏 — after the color coat dries, this band levels the two coats into one flat surface. The scratch is still visible, but the color no longer has to cross it.
- **P2000 to P3000** ✨ — de-raising and de-hazing, where the panel reads hazy under raking light. The [P3000 fine 8-hole disc](/en/disc/gray8) sits on the fine end of that band.

The whole sequence is one grit or one band coarser to finer at a time, and the panel never sees a finer grit before the coarser scratches are gone. The grit bands, the disc formats for each and the panel shape that maps to each are in the [automotive repair sanding use](/en/applications/automotive) notes, and the [sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) is where to confirm the grit and format stock before the panel work starts.


### When the panel still looks ground after the grit is right

The grit is right, the sequence is right, and the panel still reads ground under raking light. The cause is one of four, and each has a different fix:

- **The panel is wet with the previous grit's dust.** The dust settles into the wet paint and shows up as a fine haze that no grit can kill; the fix is extraction running at the panel and a dust-free store for the discs, not a wipe with a dry rag.
- **The scratch band was two steps, not one.** A P180 scratch that P800 has to kill shows through; the fix is the missing P320 or P400 step, not a finer grit on top.
- **The backing pad groove is deeper than the grit scratch.** The panel sees the pad's line, not the grit's scratch; the fix is a clean or swapped pad, not a grit change.
- **The color coat is thin in the spot.** A grit that was even shows up uneven where the color is thin; the fix is the color, not the panel, and the panel is already at the finish line.

For each of these, [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) shows the grit and format to reach for, and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) page is where to confirm the grit band is stocked before the next panel. The thread running through all four is that the panel reads ground not because the grit was wrong, but because something below the grit, the dust, the pad or the color, was not at the finish line.
