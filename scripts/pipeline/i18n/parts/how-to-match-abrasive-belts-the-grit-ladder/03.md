### When a belt should replace a flap disc, and when it should not 🔄

Belts and flap discs do overlapping work, and the honest split is: belts for sustained, straight-line or bench work where the cut has to be even over a long run; flap discs for short, high-force hand grinding where you want to press in on a spot. If your job is long, even, machine-fed work — board after board, panel after panel — a belt on the right rung beats a flap disc on nearly every cost-per-part figure, which is why a [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) account and a belt account usually feed different shops in the same factory.

- **Belt wins** — long bench or conveyor runs, even multi-panel finishing, high-volume same-grit work, and any job where the machine is doing the moving.
- **Flap disc wins** — spot grinding, weld cap knock-down in the field, and short jobs where the setup time of a belt platen would exceed the job itself.
- **Same finish, different tool** — when the finish is the goal and not the tool, spec the grit first and let the tool follow; a P240 belt and a P240 flap disc leave a comparable scratch if the pressure is honest.

If both are in the fleet, the rule of thumb is to run the belt for the long and the disc for the short, and to keep the grit standards aligned across both so the surface does not show a seam where the tooling changed. A common sight on a floor that has both is a P120 belt doing the leveling and a P120 flap disc doing the touch-up on the same part, and that is the alignment working: the rung is the spec, the tool is the delivery. When the two disagree, the grit standard on one of them is not the one you think it is, and that is the thing to check before you blame the tool.


### Specing a wholesale or OEM order 📋

When you move from buying a handful of belts to a wholesale or private label run, the conversation shifts from grit to a spec sheet, and the fields that make or break the order are the ones below. They are exactly what the [OEM and private label service page](/en/oem) walks through in more detail, and they are the same fields a [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) account uses, which is the reason a shop that runs both ends up with two clean spec sheets instead of one muddy one.

- **Grain and grit** — the grain (aluminium oxide, zirconia alumina, silicon carbide) and the FEPA grit, stated on the same scale the shop buys to.
- **Backing and bond** — cloth or paper, and the bond hardness, matched to the machine speed and the job as above.
- **Belt size and machine** — the size and the machine it will run on, so the line speed is confirmed against both before the order ships.
- **Maximum line speed** — confirmed against the model, never assumed, because it has to come from the belt and the machine.
- **Label and packing** — your logo, your part code and any spec sheet you want on the box for a private label run.
- **Quantity and lead time** — the order quantity and how fast it needs to be on the shelf, which sets whether it ships from stock or gets made to the run.

Send those six fields and the order is a conversation instead of a gamble. The [request a quotation](/en/quote) page is where to start, and if you are new to a branded run, ask for a sample against the spec before the full order so the grit, the backing and the label all match what you expect. That sample is the cheapest insurance in the whole order, and the one most shops skip.


### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on a new surface?** In many cases P60 to P80 for heavy stock and P100 to P120 for leveling; start coarse, work down one rung at a time, and the ladder does the rest.

**What is the difference between the belt grits?** The rung on the ladder: coarser removes more, finer cleans up. The grain decides how the cut feels; the grit decides how much it removes, and the two should be matched to the material before the sequence is set, not after the first complaint.

**Can I run a P120 belt on the same machine as a P60 belt?** Yes, as long as the machine speed is under the rated maximum for both, the tracking is set, and the belt is the right size for the platen. The grit does not change the machine fit; the size and the speed do.

**Why does my belt stop cutting?** Usually glazing or loading, or a grit that is finer than the job needs. Step down a rung or move to a harder grain, and keep the belt moving instead of pressing in. Worn tracking wings that let the belt drift will also cut the effective face down over a long run.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848 grit, plus the belt size, the grain and the machine the belt will run on. That is the set that keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**How do I buy abrasive belts in bulk?** Send the grit, the grain, the size, the machine and the standard to a supplier; [the abrasive belt range](/en/collections/abrasive-belts) and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form are where a bulk or branded order starts, and the [OEM and private label service page](/en/oem) is where a branded run is scoped.

**When should I switch from a flap disc to a belt?** When the job is long, even and machine-fed — board after board or panel after panel. A belt on the right rung beats a disc on nearly every cost-per-part figure at that length, which is the split that a [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) buyer and a belt buyer in the same factory usually end up with.

**What is the difference between a cloth and a paper backing on a belt?** Cloth takes the speed and heat of a fast machine; paper runs cooler and suits the light finishing runs where heat on the part is the risk. The machine speed is the call, not the grit, so match the backing to the machine before you commit a rung.


### Before you buy ✅

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm the machine's no-load speed is under the belt's printed maximum line speed before every use.
- Check the belt size matches the platen, and the tracking wings are set so the belt runs centered.
- Fit and keep the guards on for that belt size, every time.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction and the right respiratory protection for the dust load.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grain, the belt size and the machine in every order, so you do not mix scales.
- Keep belts segregated by grit, and do not run a coarser belt on a finer pass.
- Follow the belt maker's install notes, and check the splice for fraying or cracks before a long run.
- Confirm the PPE and control baseline with [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the setup guidance at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
