### FAQ

**What grit do I start on for raw panel work?** For most shop wood, P100 to P150 to even the face, and P80 where the material is heavy or the template is rough. The start is the coarsest grit the finish will allow, and the sequence from there is one step at a time down to the final pass.

**What is the difference between a loop and a lap belt?** A loop closes the two ends so the seam is short and felt rather than seen; a lap is a butt joint that passes over the platen every revolution and keeps the face fresh. The lap is for the early, aggressive work, and the loop is for the final grits, where the joint must not re-scratch the surface it just cleaned.

**How do I check the machine speed is safe for a belt?** Confirm the rated speed of the belt against the machine, and keep the machine below it. The number comes from the belt's specification, not from a guess, and it is confirmed against the model before an order ships. The PPE and machine set-up side is covered at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the practical read is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels" occupational health answers](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).

**Which wide abrasive belts should I start with on a final pass over a sealed surface?** A grit 800 belt at light pressure, opening the face for the next coat; the belt is doing the work of taking down the rub marks, not the stock. A loop joint, the right grain for the material, and the machine below the belt's rated speed.

**Can I use the same belt on wood and on metal?** Not the same grain: aluminum oxide is the wood grain, and the metal wants a grain that will not load on the slick, soft cut. The loop size may match, but the grain and the grit band do not, so the belt is changed with the material, and the sequence is rebuilt for each one.

**How long should a belt last, and what is the cost per panel?** The life is measured on your material, on your machine, at your step — the grain, the bond and the material pull apart differently in every shop, so the number is a measurement, not a quote. The cost question is the cost per square foot sanded, and the two belts you are choosing between should be run for a shift and the panels counted.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, on the grit, so the number means the same cut at your shop as at the factory. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and the standard is a field on the spec, not a footnote, which is why it is one of the things the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form asks for.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist, as a reminder and not a substitute for your own site rules. In compliant use with the right protection, the risk of a wide-belt sander is reduced but not zero, and the checklist is what the reduction is built on.

- Confirm the machine's speed is below the belt's rated speed, and the number comes from the belt's specification, before the first pass.
- Check the loop size and the joint fit the machine's platen and wheel, and the width sits centred with both edges clear.
- Keep the machine guard and fence set for the panel, and the machine stopped before the belt is touched.
- Wear eye and face protection, and the right respiratory protection for the dust load, with no loose clothing near the loop.
- Run the dust extractor at the work for the whole pass, and store the waste where it will not get re-buffed into the air.
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, the loop, the joint and the grain in every order, so the belt that arrives is the belt that was asked for.
- Ask for the inspection report or the certificate for the loop, and confirm the storage and the transport so the joint is not cut and the backing is not heat-soaked before it reaches the machine.
- Follow the maker's install notes for tension and tracking, and set both at speed, not at rest.
- Keep the PPE and the controls on record; the U.S. baseline is at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the machine set-up side is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels" occupational health answers](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
