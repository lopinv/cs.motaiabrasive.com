### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The format fails in a handful of predictable places, and the fixes are cheap:

- **Coupling reversed** 🔄 — the loop face of the disc has to meet the hook face of the plate; if the disc will not hold at speed, flip it before you blame the coupling.
- **Tool speed past the disc's rated speed** ⚠️ — the one that ends with a disc in the shop; confirm the tool maximum RPM and the disc's rated speed and keep the tool below it.
- **Skipping grits** 📏 — going from P150 straight to P320 leaves P150 scratches under the finish; add the P240 step and the surface clears.
- **Pressing into a fine grit** 🪶 — P320 and finer wants to skim; pressing makes the grain glaze, so lighter pressure and a moving pad, not a coarser grit.
- **Pad smaller than the back plate** ⭕ — without the coupling ring for that pad size the disc cannot seat and cannot be trusted at speed; use the ring or match the diameters.
- **Dusty work on a fine solid face** 🌫️ — the hole pattern on a coarse grit is what keeps the face open; on P320 and finer dry work the holeless face is the one that does not blow through.
- **Dirty back plate** 🧹 — dust and old loops packed into the hook face mean the disc will not seat and will shed; the hook comb between grits is the maintenance that keeps the format working.
- **Mixed-grit shelf** 📦 — the same P320 label on two suppliers can be two different grain sizes when the scale is not named; keep the shop to one scale and name it in every order, so the P320 you buy is the P320 you spec'd.

The thread that runs through all of these is that hook-and-loop discs fail as match problems, not as bad discs; fix the coupling, the speed, the grit sequence or the plate and the format does what it should.


### FAQ

**What grit should I buy for paint prep?** The band that does the work is P240 and P320 for the pre-paint passes, with P400 where the top coat is the goal. P320 is the bridge grit most shops stop at and P400 is where the finishing pass lands.

**When do I pick a perforated pad over a holeless one?** Perforated goes on P150 and coarser work that makes real dust, because the hole pattern keeps the face open; holeless goes on P320 and finer dry work, anywhere blow-through would land on the part. The P180 to P240 band is the judgment call and the material decides.

**Does the pad have to match the back-plate size exactly?** The diameter has to match: the pad equals the back-plate, or is smaller with the coupling ring for that pad size in hand. A pad larger than the plate is not a fit and will not hold at speed, so confirm the back-plate diameter before the pad is ordered.

**What is 320 grit actually for?** 320 grit is the classic pre-paint finishing grit: it takes P240 scratches out and leaves a face a top coat can sit on, but it is not a final polish; the P400 or P800 pass is where a panel gets to final, which is why P320 is the bridge grit, not the stop grit.

**How long does the coupling last?** The disc is single-use; the back plate and its hook face outlast it, but the hooks wear with every changeover and every cleaning. Clean the hook face with a hook comb between grits and keep it flat and dry, and the plate does far more changeovers; ask the supplier for the life figure against your changeover rate and confirm it with sales.

**How do I buy hook and loop sanding pads in bulk?** Send the grit, the face diameter, the pad style, the coupling format and the quantity to a supplier, and name the grit standard you are buying to; the [request a quotation](/quote) form and the [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) are where a bulk or branded order starts.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 grit, never a bare number, so the P320 you order is the P320 you expected; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and the term is defined at [the abrasives glossary](/glossary).


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist; in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum RPM against the disc's rated speed and keep the tool below it; the rated speed has to come from the disc, so confirm it with sales.
- Check that the pad diameter equals the back-plate diameter, or that the coupling ring for the smaller pad size is in hand.
- Clean the hook face with a hook comb and confirm the back plate is flat and dry before the first disc.
- Fit the tool guard and keep eye and face protection on for every pass.
- Run dust extraction with the right filter for the material and the right respiratory protection for the load; the U.S. baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and setup guidance is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, in the order, and confirm the grit range P60 to P5000 covers the band you need.
- Check lead time, minimum order quantity and packing for a bulk or private-label order, and start with the [request a quotation](/quote) form and the [OEM and private-label service page](/oem).
- Follow the maker's install notes on the disc, and check the part and the pad for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material.

If you share the material, the finish you are aiming for and the tool you run, the team can pre-select a hook and loop sanding pad and the grit band to start from; send the three and the sequence is worked on the [request a quotation](/quote) page.
