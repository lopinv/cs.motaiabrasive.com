### Reading the belt face before you spend a panel on it

A belt that is about to stop cutting tells you before the panel shows it, and the reading is a three-check routine that takes under a minute. The first check is the cut: take one test panel and watch how the face moves. A healthy face takes even pressure and moves stock; a tired face takes the same pressure and stops moving it, and the difference is the one you feel in the hand. The second check is the dust: the colour and the amount of dust that comes off the face is a direct read on the coat, because a packed face throws less dust and a fresh face throws more, and the cut follows the dust. The third check is the look: a face that has glazed reads glassy and pale under the light, and the patch will not come back with grit, only with a step up or a clean.

Put the three together into one habit and you can predict a belt's life instead of discovering it on a finished panel:

- **Cut, dust, look — every shift** 🔍 — one test panel at the start of the shift tells you the face is still honest; run it when the machine is quiet, not between panels.
- **Track panels per belt per size** 📊 — the number that justifies the purchase price; when a new belt dies early, the number tells you whether the grit, the coat or the speed is the cause.
- **Re-lap where the machine allows it** 🧵 — a closed belt re-lap at the start of the shift moves the peel spot and stretches the life; a lap belt, you watch the head and foot instead.

The reason the routine matters more on MDF than on a harder material is that the packed face dies early, and the early death is the one you can see coming. A steel face that slows down tells you slowly; an MDF face tells you in one panel, and the three-check habit is what catches it. ### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on an MDF panel?** In most cases P80 to P120 for the face after the saw and mill, then P150 to P240 to even it out. Start coarse, work down one step at a time, and never jump two steps on the board itself.

**What is the 1500 grit for, exactly?** The fine finishing band, from P1500 up to P2000 and beyond, is for the coats, not the board: de-nibbing and levelling dried primer and top coats. It is the last grit in the job, and it runs on the fine, paper-backed end of the scale.

**Why does my belt clog on MDF?** The dust is fine and resin-laden, so it packs the face faster than wood dust would. The fixes are an open coat at the same grit, a grit one step finer than the pass needs, and extraction at the point of work; the packed face is also why the close coat belt dies early on MDF.

**What is the difference between cloth-backed and paper-backed belts?** The cloth (X and C weights) takes pressure and heat, so it carries the coarse and medium grits; the paper (J and Y weights) is softer and sheds the fine dust better, so it carries P240 and up. Match the backing to the grit, then match the grit to the pass, not the other way round.

**Belts or discs for MDF edges and contours?** The belt is faster on any face it can reach; the random orbital with PSA or mesh discs is the tool for the last few millimetres of radius and for door edges. A furniture job usually runs both: the line or the finisher for the faces, the discs for what the belt cannot reach.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848 grit, plus the belt size and the build you are running — the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA). Naming the standard keeps your order from arriving as a different grit than the one on the shelf.

**Can I run the same belt on MDF and solid wood?** Mostly yes, on the coarse and medium grits: the grain and the backing travel fine between the two. Where it goes wrong is the fine end, because solid wood does not pack the face the way MDF does, and a fine finishing grit on bare wood is a slower job for no reason.

**How do I spec a belt for the belt line?** Send the line speed you run, the panel mix, the grit ladder you work to and the belt size, and confirm the rated speed against the model; the [1520 × 630 mm abrasive belt](/en/products/1520x630-abrasive-belt) and the [2200 × 1330 mm wide belt](/en/products/2200x1330-abrasive-belt) are the two line sizes, and the grits stocked at each are confirmed with sales at enquiry time.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. It is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of this work is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your machine's speed is below the rated speed of the belt or disc for the model you are running, and keep the machine card carrying that number.
- Check the fit before the panel goes in: belt tracking on the machine, the disc fully seated on the flange, and the guard in place, because the guard is the first barrier against a failure.
- Wear eye and face protection on every pass, and use respiratory protection sized to the dust load; the PPE selection baseline is at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132).
- Run dust extraction at the point of work, not just the room, and keep the floor of the sanding bay clear of the dust that becomes the next pass's problem.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grit, the belt size and the build in every enquiry, so the order does not arrive as a different grit than the one you tested.
- Ask for the inspection report or certificate that comes with the model you are standardising on, and check storage: belts kept off the floor, away from heat and out of the sun, with the roll standing or laid as the maker's notes say.
- Follow the install notes for the belt on your machine — tracking, tension and the re-lap point — and check the face of the panel for heat and scratch depth after the first run, not after the hundredth.
- For a belt-line order or a regular order, confirm MOQ, lead time and packing on the [OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem) and start with the [quotation form](/en/quote).
