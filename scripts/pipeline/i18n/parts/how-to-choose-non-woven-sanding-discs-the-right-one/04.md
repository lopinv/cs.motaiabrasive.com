### FAQ

**What grit of non-woven sanding discs should I start with?** For general preparation and blending, P120 to P180 covers most of the bench. Start coarser than feels comfortable, work down the ladder, and do not skip two steps at a time.

**What does a perforated face do that a solid one does not?** It gives the dust a direct exit through the disc instead of up around the edge, so the face keeps cutting longer on fine grits and heavy-dust jobs. Coarse chip-throwing work tolerates a solid face; P400 and finer want the perforated or mesh format.

**Aluminum oxide or ceramic grain on a high-volume bench?** Aluminum oxide is the predictable default and the cost answer on mixed work; ceramic grain is the per-surface answer where one grit runs all day on one material, because it lasts longer and pulls the finished-surface cost down.

**PSA or hook-and-loop, which do I buy?** If the job is one panel and done, PSA is the cheap, clean choice. If the same grit goes on the same material all day, hook-and-loop pays for itself across the reuse passes. The format has to match the pad you already run, so check that first.

**What grit standard do I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, stated in the same line as the grit number, so two suppliers quoting the same number are not quietly quoting two grain sizes. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**What is P2800 actually for?** It is a fine scuff and de-raise grit, not a cutting grit. You run it over a surface that is already close, to open it for a top coat or soften the last marks. It wants to be the final step of the sequence, with light pressure and a moving disc.

**How do I buy these in bulk or with my own label?** Send grain, grit, format, diameter, the standard and the quantity to a supplier; the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form is where a bulk order starts, and the [OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem) is where a branded run starts.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not removed.

- Confirm the tool's rated speed is below the disc's rated speed for the model you are actually ordering; where the model does not print a maximum, confirm the rated speed with sales before it ships.
- Check the arbor, pad or mandrel fit, and that the disc format — PSA, mesh, non-hole or hook-and-loop — matches what the tool actually takes.
- Keep the guard or the sander's dust path fitted and working; it is the first defence against both a disc failure and the dust load.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction with respiratory protection sized to the dust, especially on the fine grits.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) and the grain in the enquiry, so the order arrives as the cut you planned.
- Confirm the inspection report or certificate of the stock lot where your site rules require it, and keep that record with the PPE and controls you rely on.
- Store discs face-up, out of humidity, and follow the maker's installation and storage notes on the label.
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any [sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) or [private label order](/en/oem), and start the numbers at the [quotation form](/en/quote).

U.S. PPE requirements are a useful baseline at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and a practical reading of spinning-abrasive setup is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
