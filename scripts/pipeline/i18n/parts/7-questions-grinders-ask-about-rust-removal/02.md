### Q5: disc or belt on the long run

The tool shape is where the stripping job splits, and the split is the call that saves the surface on the long, flat faces.

- **The Abrasive Belts on the flat, long faces** — the belt machine takes the face, and the grit follows the disc ladder with the belt width following the face. The [abrasive belt range](/collections/all) shows the widths, and the belt is the tool for the volume face where the disc would take three passes to the belt's one.
- **The disc on the detail and the corners** — the flap disc takes the joint, the edge and the curve, and the grit is the same ladder as the belt so the two tools cut the same way.

The rule is that the tool shape follows the surface shape, and the grit follows both. A face that is flat and long is the belt's face, and a face that is detailed is the disc's face, and the grit is the same 150 to 320 ladder across both tools so the surface does not show the tool change. The Abrasive Belts family on the belt machine is the volume answer, and the disc on the grinder is the detail answer, and the two tools sharing the same grit is the split that keeps the surface honest. The [abrasive belt range](/collections/all) and the [flap disc range](/collections/flap-discs) are the two sides of the split, and the grit is the thread that runs through both. The widths to build from are the 533 by 75, 610 by 100, 1520 by 630 and 2200 by 1330 mm belt forms, and the 1520 by 630 is the one the large belt machines run for the hull and the panel face, with the 610 by 100 the standard on the bench sander for the detail work.


### Q6: what is the cost number that matters

The cost number on a stripping run is cost per surface, not price per disc, and the run is where the trap bites hardest, because the stripping step is long, the heat is high and the cheap disc is the one that glazes.

- **The disc that holds its cut** — the heat-tough grain on the demanding base, and the pass that runs to the last step without the face dulling. The unit price is higher, and the discs-per-surface is lower, and the ratio is the number that wins on the long run.
- **The disc that glazes** — the general grain on the light base, and the face that dulls by the second pass and the swap that is two a panel instead of one. The unit price is lower, and the discs-per-surface is higher, and the ratio is the number that loses.

The match test is the scrap plate: take a piece of the job material, run the candidate disc from the stripping step to the last pass, count the discs, and divide by the panels finished. Do it against the incumbent and the two come back as one ratio, and that ratio is the number that belongs in the order conversation, not the per-disc price. The [quotation form](/quote) is where the number is built, and the sample against the plate is the test that separates the disc that holds the cut from the disc that glazes, and the two are the same P-number on the box until the plate says otherwise. One habit from the shop floor: keep the scrap plate and the pass count the same for every candidate, because the comparison that matters is the surface after, and the number is the one that survives the shelf.


### Q7: what do I send in the enquiry

The fields are the order, and the supplier who answers all of them is the one to standardize on. The six, in the order the supplier reads them:

- **Grain and grit** — the grain, the grit, and the FEPA or ISO 848 standard on the same scale the shop buys to.
- **Backing** — the light base, the steel base or the glass base, chosen to the tool and the heat as above.
- **Diameter and the tool check** — the disc size the tool takes, and the rated speed of the disc against the tool's no-load speed.
- **The finish line** — the paint spec or the weld spec that the grit sequence ends at, because the sequence is built to the trade's call.
- **Quantity and lead time** — the order quantity and the shelf date, which sets whether it ships from stock or gets made to the run.
- **Label and packing** — the logo, the part code and the spec sheet on the box for a branded run.

Send those fields and the order is a conversation instead of a gamble. The [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) walks the branded run in more detail, and it is where the label and the spec sheet get built, and the [quotation form](/quote) takes the fields so the order starts with the ones that make or break it. One note on the numbers we share: we list the grain, the backing, the grit and the standard as standard, and we confirm the rated speed against each model before an order ships, because that number has to come from the disc, not from a guess. For the wholesale side, the fields are the same whether the run is a sample or the shelf, and the [quotation form](/quote) is the one place the six get sent together, because the split across emails is where a field gets dropped and the order ships wrong.
