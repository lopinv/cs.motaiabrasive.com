### Cost, life and lead time: one sandpaper manufacturer 💰

On cost, the number that matters is not the price per disc but the price per finished surface. A cheap P120 disc that lasts half as long as a good one does not save money on the panel; it doubles the changeovers, doubles the dust, and doubles the bench time, and on a volume line that is where the saving actually disappears. A disc that cuts evenly does more work per change; a flap disc that keeps cutting as it wears does more work per disc on the grinding side. Compare two products you are choosing between on cost per finished part, not sticker price, and ask the supplier to help you with the number. That is the ground the conversation should be built on, whether it is [the sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) or [the flap disc range](/collections/flap-discs), because at volume the per-part cost is where the saving lands.

Lead time has two shapes, and the order should name which one it is:

- **Stock runs** — common grits and common diameters, made and held on the shelf; these ship fast, and the question is quantity, not waiting 📦
- **Made-to-order runs** — an off-grid grit, a backing not in the stock mix, or a label that is not on the line yet; these take longer because the grain, the backing and the label all have to line up in one production pass, and the date moves with the run 🗓️

For an OEM and private label job, the lead time and the minimum order are the two numbers that move the plan. The [OEM & private-label service page](/oem) walks through what goes on the label, the packing and the spec sheet — your logo, your part code, your grit list — and the minimum order quantity is set against the run, not the unit, so confirm the MOQ with sales before a date is promised anywhere. A sample against the spec before the full order is the move that keeps a branded run from arriving wrong: the grit, the backing and the label all match what was asked for, and the full order lands on schedule. For the making side of all of this, [the manufacturing & quality page](/factory) is where the process and the checks live, and the [request a quotation](/quote) page is where the spec starts. On paperwork, the inspection report and the certificate set that ship with the order are fields on the quote, not assumptions — name what you need in the enquiry and it comes with the goods.


### Common mistakes and how to fix them 🛠️

Most of the complaints that reach the bench are match problems, not product problems, and the fixes are usually cheaper than the mistake. In the order the mistakes show up:

- **Skipping a grit** ⏭️ — P80 straight to P400 leaves P80 scratches under the finish. Add the P180 to P240 step and the surface clears; the rule is one step at a time, not two.
- **Glazing and loading** 🫥 — the face packs with soft material, or the grit lays flat and stops cutting. Drop a grit, move to a harder grain where the material wants it, and clean the face; pressing harder is the opposite of the fix, and it is the first move everyone makes.
- **Edge-peel on the hook-and-loop** 🔗 — the disc lifts at the rim under pressure and the sanding goes uneven at the edge. Confirm the sander plate is clean and the format matches the tool; if the edges keep peeling on a clean plate, state it back to the supplier against the term in [the abrasives glossary](/glossary) and hold the lot to the spec.
- **Speed mismatch** ⚡ — a flap disc run past its printed maximum, or a disc format on a tool it was not made for. Read both labels before the first cut, and where a number is missing, confirm the rated speed with sales and keep the tool below it.
- **A flap doing fine work** ✨ — the directional texture the flaps leave will not come out of the surface at P600 and finer without a disc pass on top. If the finish target is coating-ready, plan the disc pass; do not hope the flap will clean itself up.
- **Wet-and-dry with the wrong format** 💧 — a standard PSA paper disc under water delaminates. The formats made for the job are the mesh discs and the water-resistant paper, and the grit is the grit either way — what changes is the backing, not the cut.
- **Two grit scales on one shelf** 📏 — a P120 and a 120 from two sources can be two grain sizes. Keep the shop on one scale, name it in every enquiry, and treat [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA) as the standing rule on the shelf.
- **Buying the grit, not the sequence** 🧮 — a P240 disc bought for a panel that is still sitting on P80 scratches is buying the wrong rung of the ladder. The grit is a step, not a destination, and the sequence is what gets quoted on the order.

The thread through all eight: the disc or the flap is only as good as the grit, the grain, the format and the speed it was matched to before the job started. Most of the complaints are mismatch complaints wearing a grit's name, and the fix is on the match, not on the product.
