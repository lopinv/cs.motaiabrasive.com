### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The panel-bench complaints that keep coming back, each with the fix that actually moves it:

- **Glazing** — the face stops cutting and starts smearing. The grain is soft against the board, or the pressure is too high. Go one grit coarser, drop the pressure, and keep the disc moving instead of holding it on one spot.
- **Loading on MDF** — dust packs the face and the disc stops biting. An open-coat disc keeps cutting through the load, and [the abrasive belt range](/collections/abrasive-belts) has the belt equivalents for the edge work that follows.
- **Skipping a grit** — P80 straight to P400 leaves P80 lines under the finish. Put P120 and P180 back in the sequence and the lines clear out one step at a time.
- **Edge lift on a veneer panel** — pressure at the edge of a thin face lifts the veneer and starts a delamination. Lighter pressure, an even hand, and a grit no coarser than P120 on the face keep the edge inside what the panel can take.
- **Speed mismatch** — a tool spinning a disc past its rated speed, or a disc that is too large for the tool's rating. Confirm the rated speed against the tool and drop the diameter, not the grit, when the face is delicate.
- **Swirl marks in the finish** — the disc stayed in one direction through two grits. Change the sanding direction between grits and the marks run out of the panel instead of circling in it.
- **Finish adhesion failure** — a coat that lifts from the board. The cause is usually P120 dust still in the P320 pass; dust off between grits, or run extraction at the work point and let the face go clean into the coat.

The thread through all seven: **the problem is almost always a match between grit, grain, pressure and direction, not a bad disc.** Fix the match and the cut comes back, which is the same rule [woodworking sanding use](/applications/woodworking) notes work through by application.


### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on an MDF panel heading for paint?** P80 to clear the topcoat and level the face, P120 to even, P320 before the coat. Keep the disc moving through the sequence, because MDF dust loads a face faster than hardwood dust does.

**Is 80 grit enough for a strip-down job?** For stripping a multi-year varnish off a panel, P60 takes the bulk of the coat and P80 levels what P60 leaves. 80 grit alone works on a single topcoat; on a heavy build, start at P60 and step down to it.

**What is the full grit sequence before a panel goes under a clear lacquer?** P80, P120, P180, P320, and P400 on the final band if the film is going to be thin. Dust off between every grit, because the dust from the coarser step sits in the face under the next coat.

**PSA sheet or hook-and-loop disc — which for a panel bench?** Hook-and-loop for a random-orbiter bench, because the grit swap takes seconds and a panel sequence wants fast swaps. PSA for a bonded pad that runs long passes on a big panel, where the disc has to stay anchored. [The 5-inch PSA sanding disc](/products/5-inch-psa-sanding-disc) is the middle size for most panel work.

**How many grits should a panel line stock?** Three for a repaint job — P80, P120, P320 — plus the belt grit for edges. Add P400 for thin films and P600 and finer for glassy fill work. The range in [the sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) covers P60 through P5000, so the bench can hold the whole ladder.

**What grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, and the scale to order to — the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA). A P120 on one scale and a 120 on another are not always the same grain size, and naming the standard is what keeps the order from mixing.

**How long does a disc last on MDF versus hardwood?** MDF wears a disc down faster per pass, because the board is softer and the dust load is heavier, and the open coat keeps cutting through that load for longer. Hardwood wears slower, but a dense board takes a tougher grain. [The abrasive belt range](/collections/abrasive-belts) has the belt equivalent of both.

**When do I stop using discs and move to a belt?** For edges, for thickness stock, and for any panel line where the face grits have done their work and the board is going to a coating station. The [Y399 abrasive belt](/model/y399) and [the 610 × 100 mm abrasive belt](/products/610x100-abrasive-belt) are the two faces we see most at that hand-off.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist for a panel bench. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant speed, the right fit and the right PPE, the risk of panel sanding is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm the tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed rated working speed before every panel pass.
- Confirm the pad, mandrel and guard are the right size for the disc you are running, that the disc seats fully on the pad, and that the belt guards are on at both infeed and outfeed on a belt station.
- Wear eye and face protection on every pass, and match the respiratory protection to the dust load of the board in front of you — hardwood, MDF and melamine are different loads.
- Run dust extraction at the point of sanding, not just a general-room fan, and plan where the collected dust goes.
- Name the grit standard in every order — FEPA or ISO 848 — so two suppliers do not quietly ship two different grain sizes.
- Ask for the certification or inspection report that sits behind the disc lot you are buying, and keep it with the order.
- Store discs flat, away from moisture, and check the packing on arrival before the first panel runs.
- Follow the maker's install notes for the backing you are running, and confirm the rated speed against the model before the tool spins.
