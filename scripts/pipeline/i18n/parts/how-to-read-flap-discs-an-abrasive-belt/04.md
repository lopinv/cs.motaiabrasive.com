### FAQ

**What grit do I start with for general grinding?** In many cases P80 to P120 covers everyday grinding and deburring on steel, then step down to P240 or P400 for blending and finish. Start coarse, work down, and do not jump two rungs at a time.

**What is the difference between a steel backing and a plastic backing disc?** A steel backing is rigid and takes pressure, so it is the pick for heavy removal and weld cap work; a plastic backing is lighter, runs cooler and suits finishing passes where you want the least heat on the part. Fiberglass sits between them for fast, hot tools. Pick by how hard the job is and how fast the tool runs.

**Can I run a 180 mm disc on the same grinder as a 125 mm disc?** Only if the guard, the flange and the tool speed all suit the larger diameter, and the no-load speed stays under the disc's printed maximum — a larger disc reaches a higher surface speed on the same machine. When in doubt, check the tool rating and the disc label before you fit it.

**Why does my disc stop cutting?** Usually loading or glazing, or a grit too fine for the material, or a tool running slower than the disc was designed for. Step the grit, change the grain, clean the flaps, and keep the disc moving rather than forcing it into the work.

**Belt or flap disc on a large, flat area?** Use the flap disc to knock down welds and high spots, then a belt at a matching grit to bring the whole area to an even finish; the loop holds one scratch pattern over a long run in a way a disc cannot. A 1520 × 630 belt is the standard bench choice for that pass.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 grit plus the diameter and arbor, so your order cannot be mixed with a different scale; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**How do I buy flap discs in bulk, or on a private label?** Send the grit, the backing, the diameter, the arbor and the standard, and the conversation becomes a spec sheet instead of a gamble; the [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) route and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form are where a bulk or branded order starts, and the OEM page covers what we can put on your label.

**Do I need a grit finer than P400 for a coated finish?** When the finish line is a high-gloss clear coat or a fine film, yes — the 1200 band de-raises and keeps the surface in one texture, and it is the rung you run last, with light pressure and a moving disc.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's no-load speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed, and the belt's line speed below its rated maximum, before every use.
- Check the arbor hole and the flange are the correct size and that the disc sits fully on the flange; use a rated adapter where needed, never a filed hole.
- Fit the guard and keep it on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc that fails.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction with respiratory protection sized to the dust load on dry grinding.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grit, the backing, the diameter and the arbor in every order so scales do not mix.
- Ask for the inspection report or test basis behind any certification you rely on, and confirm the maximum working speed against the specific model rather than assuming it.
- Store the stock dry and cool, keep spares in their original packing, and transport the cartons away from the grinding dust; coated abrasives take up moisture and lose cut.
- Follow the maker's install notes for the flange, the adapter and the guard size, and on thin or soft material check the part for heat and scratch depth early in the pass.

The U.S. requirements are a useful baseline at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the setup side of abrasive wheels is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels" occupational health answers](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
