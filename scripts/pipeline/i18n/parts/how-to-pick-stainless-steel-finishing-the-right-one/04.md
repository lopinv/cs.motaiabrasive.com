### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist, and a reminder that under compliant use, with the right protection, the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm the tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed, for every diameter you plan to run on it.
- Check the arbor hole and the flange are the correct size, and use a rated adapter where a setup needs one.
- Fit the guard at the correct size for the disc and keep it on for every pass.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction and the respiratory protection matched to the dust load for dry grinding.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every order, along with the grit, the backing, the diameter, and the arbor.
- Ask for the inspection report or certificate against the specific model, and keep the PPE and control records the job relies on.
- Store the abrasive dry, keep the stainless set separate from the mild-steel set, and check the transport packing for damage before the run starts.
- Follow the install notes for the tool and the disc, and check the panel for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft stock.

For the PPE and setup side, the U.S. baseline is [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the practical reading is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels). For the order, the [request a quotation](/en/quote) page is where a stock run or a branded run starts, and the [all abrasive products](/en/collections/all) page is where the grit, the back, and the size are matched before the conversation with sales.

If you share the material, the finish you are aiming for, and the tool you run, the team can pre-select the grit and the backing to start from, and the sequence works out from there.
