### FAQ

**What grit do I start on a Stainless Steel Finishing joint's cap?** P80 to P100 on a steel-backed disc, down to flush. Then the ladder: P120 to even, P240 to blend the line, P400 to clean up. The cap is where pressure matters more than the grit, and the grits in that band are in [the flap disc range](/en/collections/flap-discs).

**Can I keep using the same disc after a carbon steel job?** No. Carbon residue in the grain is a contamination source on stainless, and the disc retires from the stainless set once it has touched both. If you run a shared set, plan on a dedicated stainless set instead, because the redo cost of a rust bloom outweighs the set cost fast.

**When do I need P2400 at all?** When the part is near-mirror, or when the customer inspects in raking light and the P400 band still reads. P2400 de-raises the whole surface so that band disappears. It is not a replacement for P400; it runs on top of a surface that is already at P400, and confirm with sales that it is stocked for your disc.

**Which backing for a fast finishing pass on stainless?** Fiberglass where the tool runs fast and the disc would overheat on plastic, steel where the cap still needs pressure, and plastic for the cooler, gentler fine pass on thin material. Match the backing to the speed, and confirm the rated speed against the model before you commit.

**Do I use a steel-backing disc for the whole Stainless Steel Finishing sequence?** No. Steel backing earns its keep on the coarse end, cap and heavy deburring. The blend and the cleanup run cooler and kinder on plastic, and the fine end of the sequence benefits from that. The three backings and where each fits are laid out in the [flap disc range](/en/collections/flap-discs) and the [metal fabrication application notes](/en/applications/metal-fabrication).

**Why do rust spots keep showing on parts that looked clean?** Embedded carbon steel residue, almost always from a disc or a tool that ran both materials. The dedicated set is the fix, not a grit change. If the set is already dedicated and you are still seeing it, check the tool: a shared angle grinder on two materials is the usual hidden source.

**Which standard do I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, and keep to one of them across every order from every supplier, so the numbers on your shelf stay comparable. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and naming it is one of the fields the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form asks for.

**How do I compare two suppliers on a finishing set?** On grit bands in stock, backing options, the standard the grits are quoted on, lead time to the very fine end, and minimum order quantities, and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) page is where those five land. The [all abrasive products](/en/collections/all) page is the catalogue side of the same question.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed before every run; when in doubt, run no-load and measure.
- Check the arbor hole and the flange are the right size, and use a rated adapter where the hole does not match, never a modified one.
- Fit and keep the guard on, sized for that disc, every shift; it is the first defence against a disc failure.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction at the work with respiratory protection matched to the metal dust load, on dry stainless work especially.
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, in every enquiry, and keep the shop on one scale so grits on your shelf stay comparable.
- Ask for the inspection report or certificate against any order that claims a standard, and confirm the grit, backing and maximum speed line up with the label before the order ships.
- Store the abrasive set dry, away from the carbon steel grits, and keep the transport packing on a disc that has run wet or dusty until it is re-checked.
- Follow the maker's install notes on the flange, the guard and the disc seating, and re-check the guard and the arbor each shift on a shop with several tools.
- Confirm lead time and minimum order quantity against any [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) or [private label order](/en/oem), and start from the [quotation form](/en/quote).
