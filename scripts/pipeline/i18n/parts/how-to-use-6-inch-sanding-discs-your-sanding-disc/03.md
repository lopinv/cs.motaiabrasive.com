### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The fixes are cheaper than the mistakes. What shows up most on the floor, with the move that actually clears it:

- **Skipping grits.** A P120 followed straight by P400 leaves P120 scratches under the coat, and they show through wherever the light rakes. Add the P240 step and the surface clears.
- **Pressing into a fine grit.** A fine disc wants to skim; the moment you lean in, the face glazes and the cut stalls. Lighter pressure and a moving disc put the cut back, and a coarser grit is the wrong answer.
- **Running past the rated speed.** If the tool's speed is above the disc's printed maximum, drop the tool speed or move to a smaller diameter, and confirm the rated speed against the model rather than assume it.
- **The wrong pad for the disc.** A loop disc on a bare pad, or a paper disc forced onto a loop pad, walks and cuts unevenly. Match the two, and clean the pad's face between grits, because P80 dust in a P400 pass is a mark you can feel.
- **A fine grit as a first pass.** A P2800 over a coarse scratch pattern works like a buffer instead of a sander. The fine grit closes scratches; it does not open the surface.
- **Trusting a number without a standard.** A grit bought on two different scales and stacked on the same shelf is a mismatch waiting to happen. Buy to one standard, FEPA or ISO 848, and say so on the shelf label.

The thread through all of them: under cutting or a bad finish is almost always a match problem, not a bad disc. Fix the grit, the backing, the pad or the speed, and the surface comes back. For the grit and backing to reach for on each material, the [woodworking sanding use](/en/applications/woodworking) and [automotive repair sanding use](/en/applications/automotive) notes give the sequence by material, and [the abrasives glossary](/en/glossary) keeps the terms straight so you and the team are diagnosing the same problem.


### FAQ

**What is the difference between a PSA disc and a loop disc?** A PSA disc is one part: the abrasive face, the paper or film backing and the adhesive that grabs a bare pad; it works, and it comes off. A loop disc snaps to a matching pad and comes off again, so one pad takes many grits. If you change grit often, the loop setup wins on time; if you run one grit on one job, the plain disc is simpler to buy and simpler to keep.

**Do 6 Inch Sanding Discs work on wood?** Yes — a 6-inch disc on a random orbital sander is an everyday workhorse on bare and finished wood. Wood wants softer pressure than metal and the grit steps up faster, because a coarse grit on soft wood cuts deeper than it feels. The sequences in the [woodworking sanding use](/en/applications/woodworking) notes start at P80 to P120 for stock removal and close at P240 to P320 for a finish line.

**Which grit do I start with on a painted panel?** P120 to P150 through the filler, P240 through the sealer, P320 to P400 before the final coat. One step at a time, and the next grit has to close the scratches the last one left; the sequence in the panel example above is the shape to copy.

**Why does my disc stop cutting?** Three of four times it is loading or glazing, or the grit is finer than the job wants. Soft, sugary dust packs the face, and a fine grit parked in one spot burns flat. The fix is lighter pressure, a moving disc, and a step to a grit that suits the material; the grain matters too, because a tougher grain keeps its face open on a material that packs.

**Does the range go above P2000?** Yes — grits run from 60 up to 5000 on the sizes we list, so 2800 sits inside the range, and the fine end is stocked for closing work and final coats. Confirm the fine grit is on the backing you need with sales before the order.

**Are mesh discs really dustless?** They run with less airborne dust than a closed paper face, because the weave lets the dust pass instead of throwing it, and on a sander with extraction the difference is the whole job. "Dustless" is a working word, not a promise of no dust at the operator: the extraction and the PPE still carry the rest.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, with the grit, the size, the backing and the grain on the same line. The full list of the terms is in [the abrasives glossary](/en/glossary), and a 6-inch pad with a coarse to fine set is a reasonable first order from [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs).

**Can I run a 6-inch disc on a smaller sander?** No — the pad size is the pair, and a 6-inch disc on a 4-inch pad bridges the edge and cuts unevenly. Confirm the pad first; the disc diameter is a spec on its own, and the tool's pad is the other half. The sizes and their pads are listed on [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs).


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist before you buy 6 Inch Sanding Discs. A reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not removed.

- ✅ Confirm your sander's rated speed is at or below the disc's rated speed, and read both labels before the first pass; where the two numbers are not in front of you, confirm the rated speed against the model.
- 🔩 Match the pad to the disc: a 6-inch pad for a 6-inch disc, the loop pad on the loop disc, the bare pad on the plain paper disc, and the centre hole aligned with the sander's port so the extraction path stays open.
- 🥽 Fit and keep the tool's guard in place, and wear eye and face protection on every pass; on a heavy dust load, add respiratory protection matched to the material, with the U.S. baseline at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132).
- 🌫️ Plan the dust route before the first pass: extraction at the work, a bin or store for the waste, and a centre-hole or mesh disc where the dust load is heaviest.
- 🔍 Ask for the grit, the size, the backing and the grain on the quote, and for an inspection report or spec confirmation on a batch run; for anything you are not certain of, confirm the details with sales.
- 📦 Store the discs flat, away from heat and moisture, and transport them so the faces are not crushed; a creased face cuts uneven from the first pass.
- 🔧 Follow the maker's install notes on the pad and the tool, and re-check the seating each shift rather than at the start of the week.
- 📋 Name the grit standard in every enquiry, FEPA or ISO 848, with the grit, the size, the backing and the grain, so two suppliers are quoting you the same disc.
