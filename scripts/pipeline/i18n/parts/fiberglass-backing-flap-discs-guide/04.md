### FAQ

**What is the grit band for fiberglass backing flap discs?** In most steel and stainless work the band runs P80 to P120 for the grinding and deburring, P240 to P400 for the blend and cleanup, and P4000 where the surface is going under a finish. Pick the band to the job, not to the tool.

**Do I need a different disc for a 100 mm and a 125 mm tool?** The grit does not change, but the diameter and the arbor do, and the line speed does. A 125 mm disc at a given RPM works faster than a 100 mm disc at the same RPM, so confirm the 125 mm disc's maximum against the tool before you fit it. The [fiberglass backing flap disc](/en/flap/fiberglass) page lists the diameters we stock.

**Can I run the same grit on a plastic and a glass disc?** You can, and the grit is the grit, but the backing changes how the disc works. The glass is firmer and wants speed; the plastic is cooler and more forgiving on a slow tool. The same P120 will cut differently on the two, and the finish will show it.

**What grain on a stainless weld?** Aluminium oxide as the default, zirconia alumina if the face is packing or the cap is thick. Match the grain to the material first, then the grit to the depth of cut.

**What is P4000 actually for?** It is the leveling grit. It does not remove the weld; it levels the last light scratch and the last coarse mark so the surface sits even under a paint or a coat. Use it on a clean disc, after the P400 pass, and do not jump to it from P120.

**Why does the back of my disc get hot?** The flaps are doing their work and the heat is normal on a sustained grinding pass. It stops being normal when the back is too hot to hold or the disc is spinning slow enough that the flaps have splayed. Raise the speed or drop a diameter, and check the printed maximum against the tool.

**How do I buy fiberglass flap discs in bulk?** Send the grit, the backing, the diameter, the arbor, and the grit standard you are buying to; the [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) page is where the conversation starts, and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form is where it lands. If you run through a reseller, the [abrasive distributor](/en/collections/all) shelf is the starting point.

**What is the arbor hole I should order?** Match it to the tool's flange. A common 22.23 mm hole on a disc that runs on a different flange is a mismatch, and the fix is a rated adapter, not a ground-down hole. Confirm the flange size before the order ships.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's no-load speed is below the disc's printed maximum line speed before every use; the number comes from the label, not the memory.
- Check the arbor hole and the flange are the correct size, and use a rated adapter where the two do not match.
- Fit and keep the guard on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc that fails.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction and the right respiratory protection for the dust load on the material you are grinding.
- Confirm grit, backing, diameter, arbor and the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every order, so a P120 on one scale does not arrive as a coarser P120 on another.
- Follow the disc maker's install notes, and check the part for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material before you commit to the sequence.
- Keep a record of the PPE and the controls you rely on; U.S. requirements are a useful baseline at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the setup side is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any [flap discs wholesale](/en/collections/flap-discs) or private-label run, and start the order on the [quotation form](/en/quote).
