### The one rule to use with Sandpaper Wholesale

Here is the decision rule that governs nearly every sandpaper wholesale conversation: **pick the grit from how far the surface is from its finish line, then pick the backing format from how you actually hold the disc against that surface.** A panel two coats from clear sits on the 1200 to 2000 band; a rough cast deck lid starts on P80; a walnut worktop lands at P320 to P400 for its final pass. That grit choice is the number that has to be right, because it sets the whole sequence. The format choice is second: a hook-and-loop disc suits a random orbital you run daily, a PSA disc suits a job where you buy one grit at a time, and a mesh disc suits curved surfaces that a flat disc bridges over.

Two scenarios where that rule bends. One: very soft, sugary materials like MDF load a disc fast, so the sequence has to run a grit coarser than you would on hard wood and keep the tool moving. Two: thin panels and thin sheet work. There, heat and cross scratches matter more than removal rate, so you drop a grit and you add a step instead of pressing harder.

If your job is everyday panel prep, wood finishing or general sanding, the working starting point is P120 on primer, P320 to P400 on finish coats, and P1200 to P2000 for the final blocking pass before clear or paint. Two habits turn this from guesswork into a repeatable spec: always state the grit standard you are buying to, in practice FEPA or ISO 848, and always confirm the disc format and the tool before stock ships. ### Choosing grit, grain and bond for a wholesale spec

Three variables do all the work on a coated disc: the grain, the grit, and the bond. Each trades against the other two, and the trades are what a wholesale spec is really about.

**Grit sets the cut.** The grit number is a FEPA or ISO 848 designation, and the scale runs the way most people expect: the higher the number, the finer the grain and the lighter the cut. The practical ladder we see across wholesale enquiries runs like this:

- **P60 – P120** 🪓 — heavy removal: old paint, mill scale, rough stock, high spot knockdown on a fresh panel.
- **P150 – P240** 📏 — general blending, primer filling, bringing a surface even before finer work.
- **P320 – P400** 🎯 — the everyday finishing band on wood and on painted surfaces: smooth, low mark, ready for the next coat.
- **P800 – P1200** 🧊 — paint and clear blocking: de-raising the surface, killing the last visible scratches, refining under a top coat.
- **P1500 – P2500** ✨ — fine blocking and pre-polish, keeping the whole surface in one texture band.
- **P3000 – P5000** 💎 — polish work, cutting compound scratches, the last pass on a panel.

We stock grits from P60 to P5000 on our sanding discs, so a full sequence from rough to polish can come off one programme. The one number people forget: the scale. FEPA numbers and older ANSI numbers do not always agree, so a P120 disc and a 120-grit disc from two different sources can carry different grain sizes even though the labels look identical. Name the standard in the enquiry; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**Grain sets the feel of the cut.** For most wholesale orders, the grain is **Aluminum Oxide** — tough, economical and the default on both metal and wood, which is why the standard coated **abrasive paper** in our range is an Aluminum Oxide grain. The options we work with:

- **Aluminum oxide** ✅ — the all rounder on wood, primed panels and general prep. Slow to load, even cut, the grain to default to when you are not chasing the fastest cut.
- **Zirconia alumina** 🔥 — tougher, more open, cuts harder on packing jobs like castings and high-removal passes.
- **Silicon carbide** ⚡ — the grain for glass, non-ferrous metal and some composites, where it cuts slicker materials without loading.
- **Ceramic** 🏅 — the premium, longest life option, where cost per finished part matters more than cost per disc.

The rule underneath both lists: **grain first, then grit.** Match the grain to the material, then match the grit to the cut. Change one deliberately and hold the other fixed, or you are solving two problems at once. That split is the one that keeps a Sandpaper Wholesale spec consistent across every repack and restock.
