### When the surface looks sanded but is not

Under-sanding on MDF has a specific look, and it is worth calling out on its own because it is the complaint that keeps panels off the coating line. The face looks matte and even to the hand, and the scratch is uniform, but under raking light the panel still shows the scratch of a rung it should have erased. The causes are one of these four, and each has a different fix:

- **The previous rung was not cleared.** The P180 pass ran over a P150 scratch it was not cut to erase, so the scratch is under the P180 texture rather than gone. Go back a rung, clear it, and come forward again.
- **The face is loading.** A dull, loaded belt erases unevenly, so some of the panel takes the P180 scratch and some of it still shows P150. Change the belt, not the grit.
- **The machine is running too fast for the belt.** A face that is not cutting at the grit it is supposed to is often a speed problem, not a grit problem; check the line speed against the plate before blaming the belt.
- **The panel moved during the pass.** A panel that shifts under the belt leaves a band of scratch at the two grits that were running at the same time; hold the feed steady and the band disappears.

The raking-light test is the cheap one: put the panel under a shallow light angle and look for the scratch of the rung you meant to erase, not the scratch of the rung you are on. If the old scratch shows, the panel is not at the rung you think it is, and the next rung will inherit it. For the tooling side, [the 610 × 100 mm abrasive belt](/en/products/610x100-abrasive-belt) shows the grits and constructions to match to the rung, and the [OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem) is where a branded run gets the spec sheet that keeps the grit, the label and the panel all agreeing.


### Specing a bulk or OEM order: the fields

When you move from buying a handful of belts to a wholesale or private label order, the conversation shifts from grit to a spec sheet. The fields that make or break an order are the ones below, and they are exactly what the [OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem) walks through in more detail.

- **Grain and grit** — the grain (aluminium oxide for the default MDF run, zirconia alumina for the open heavy- loading runs) and the FEPA or ISO 848 grit, stated on the same scale you shop to.
- **Construction and size** — the belt size (610 × 100 mm is the mid-volume MDF size, and the [Y399 abrasive belt](/en/model/y399) is the model it maps to), the loop length, and the construction, confirmed against the machine.
- **Grit standard** — FEPA or ISO 848, named on the spec so the stock and the order are on the same scale.
- **Label and packing** — your logo, your part code and any spec sheet you want on the box for a private label run.
- **Quantity and lead time** — the order quantity and how fast it needs to be on the shelf, which sets whether it ships from stock or gets made to your run.
- **The rung it will run** — the grit this belt is doing on the ladder, so the label and the grit on the shelf are not out of step with the panel work.

Send those fields and the order is a conversation instead of a gamble. The [request a quotation](/en/quote) page is where to start, and if you are new to a branded run, ask for a sample against the spec before the full order so the grit, the label and the panel work all match what you expect.


### FAQ

**What grit do I start MDF sanding on?** For a fresh panel with saw marks, P80 or P100 on the opening rung. For a panel that came through a machine and just needs cleanup, P120 or P150. The opening grit is set by how much material is on the face, not by the finish line.

**What is P180 for on an MDF panel?** P180 is the preparation rung: it removes the P150 micro scratch and leaves the texture a primer wants to sit on. It is the last abrasive a panel meets before it goes to the primer table on a painted MDF job.

**How many belts do I need on the shelf for a furniture run?** Three earn their keep: P100, P180 and P240. P150, P120 and P320 can be stocked on demand, and the split keeps the shelf honest without holding every rung in the ladder at the same depth.

**What is the difference between a wide belt and a 610 × 100 belt?** The wide belt, at the [2200 × 1330 mm wide belt](/en/products/2200x1330-abrasive-belt) size, is where the panel volume lives; it is the big loop for the big machine. The 610 × 100 is the mid-volume and detail size, and both run the same grit ladder on the same speed rule.

**Why does my panel still show scratches after P180?** The P150 scratch was not cleared before the P180 pass, the face loaded, or the panel moved during the pass. Check the raking light, change the belt if it is dull, and hold the feed steady.

**What standard do I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, plus the grit, the size, the construction and the loop length. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and naming the standard is what keeps the stock and the order on the same scale.

**What PPE does MDF sanding actually need?** Eye and face protection on belt work, dust extraction at the table, and respiratory protection matched to the dust load. The U.S. baseline is at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the dust reading is at [abrasive dust and airborne particulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust).

**How do I buy MDF sanding belts in bulk?** Send the grit, the size, the construction, the loop length, the standard and the quantity; the [abrasive belt range](/en/collections/abrasive-belts) and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) page are where the bulk or branded order starts.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your machine's no-load speed and the belt's maximum line speed at the loop length you are running, and keep the machine at or under the belt's rated speed.
- Check the belt is fully seated on the platen, the loop length is set for the machine, and the guards are in place and in good condition.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction at the table with the right respiratory protection for the MDF dust load.
- Confirm the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grit, the size, the construction and the loop length in every order so you do not mix scales or sizes.
- Check the certs and the inspection report for the belt before the first run on a new machine, and follow the belt maker's install notes for the loop and the tracking.
- Store the belts away from the dust and the heat of the table, and plan the storage and transport so the face is not loaded before the run.
- Keep a record of the PPE and the controls you rely on; U.S. requirements are a useful baseline at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and [OSHA portable power tools](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.244), and setup guidance is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any bulk or [private label order](/en/oem), and start with the [quotation form](/en/quote).
