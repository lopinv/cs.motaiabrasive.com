### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on a raw panel?** In many cases P120 to P180 covers the first step on raw wood or a primer knockdown, then step down through P240 to P320 before you get into the finishing band. Start coarse, work down, and never jump two steps at a time.

**How do I pick the right pad for my random orbital sander?** Match the pad diameter to the back plate diameter, and match the style to the machine: PSA or hook-and-loop for an orbital, a perforated pad for a through-flow extractor, a holeless pad where you want a continuous sheet on a firm, steady pass. Confirm the tool's maximum speed against the pad's rated speed and run at the lower of the two.

**When do I actually use P800?** P800 is the last de-nib grit before the topcoat on a two-coat paint system. It clears the small defects P400 leaves and keeps the scratch pattern uniform. It is not a first pass, and on wood or laminate you commonly jump past it to P600 or P1000.

**Will a 6 inch pad fit my 5 inch machine?** No — the pad has to sit flat on a back plate of the same diameter. A 6 inch pad on a 5 inch plate overhangs and the corners curl, which is a scratch ring on the part and a safety issue at speed. Confirm the diameter against the back plate before you order, and keep [the sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) open when you are picking.

**How do I know when to change the pad?** Two signs: the cut has gone soft and the face looks flat and shiny instead of open, or the edges are lifting and you are getting a scratch ring on the panel. Either way the fix is the same: replace the pad and re-check the style against the back plate.

**What grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 grit plus the diameter and the pad style; that keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**Can I buy sanding pads on your private label?** Yes — the [OEM & private-label service page](/oem) covers what we can put on the label and the packing, and the [request a quotation](/quote) form is where a bulk or branded order starts. Confirm the minimum order quantity with sales before you commit a run.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the pad's rated maximum working speed before every use; that figure comes from the pad maker, so confirm it against the model.
- Match the pad diameter to the back plate diameter, and use a pad style (PSA, perforated or holeless) that the machine is built to run.
- Fit and keep the tool guard on at all times, and check the pad sits flat with no corners lifting.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction at the work with a respirator that suits the dust load for the material.
- Confirm the grit, the pad style, the diameter and the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every order so you do not mix scales.
- Ask for the inspection record or certificate against the lot number you are buying; that is the record a [request a quotation](/quote) conversation should carry.
- Store pads dry and away from heat, and keep the grits separated by band so a P60 does not find its way into a P400 drawer.
- Follow the pad maker's install notes, and on a soft panel check the surface for heat and scratch depth between grit steps.
