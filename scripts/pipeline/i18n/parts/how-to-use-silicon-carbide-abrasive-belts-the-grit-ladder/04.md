### FAQ

**What grit do I start on an aluminium part?** On most raw aluminium, P80 is the start for stock removal, and P40 is the one you reach for when there is a lot of rough surface to move. The ladder from there is P120, P240, P400 and the finer steps to the finish target, and the grain for the whole sequence is SiC.

**Will a silicon carbide belt work on stainless?** It cuts stainless, but it packs on a ferrous surface faster than it should, and the blend-and-clean work on stainless is usually better on another grain family. Use SiC on stainless where the previous step was already on SiC and you need the same grain to finish the mark, and see the [stainless steel finishing use](/applications/stainless-steel) notes for the rest of the sequence.

**What does the top polish step do that P400 does not?** The polish pass does not remove stock; it de-raises. P400 is the clean-up step, and the polish step is the one on top of it, and the two are run in that order, not the other way around. A P400 scratch that was never sanded out will show under the polished face, and the fix is the missing middle step, not the polish grit.

**How many belts do I need for a batch of parts?** The number is the cost-per-surface one, and it is the one to ask a supplier to work out against your material and your finish target, because a belt on the matched job runs longer than a belt on the job it is not matched to. For a steady batch, the [request a quotation](/quote) conversation is where that number gets set, and it is the one that carries the grain, the grit and the size.

**Which standard do I name in an enquiry?** The FEPA or ISO 848 grit, the grain, the size, the backing and the bond — the fields, on the one scale the shop is built on. Naming the standard is the move that keeps the order from arriving on the wrong grit scale, and the reference for the scale is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**Which belt size do I use for a hand tool and a bench machine?** The size is the one the tool is built for, and the speed is the one the tool is rated for, and the two are checked against the belt's rated speed, not the other way around. The [610 × 100 mm abrasive belt](/products/610x100-abrasive-belt) is the bench-machine class, and the wide sizes are the machine class, and the [2200 × 1330 mm wide belt](/products/2200x1330-abrasive-belt) is where the machine class starts.

**Why does my belt stop cutting on a job it used to cut?** The two answers are the match and the pressure. A belt that is the wrong grain family for the material goes dull before the grit does, and a belt that is pressed into the work on the fine steps glazes and stops cutting. The fix is the grain and the light pressure, and the six-mistake list above is where each one is worked through.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum no-load speed is at or below the belt's printed rated maximum working speed before the belt goes on; where a speed is not printed, confirm the rated speed against the model and keep the tool below it.
- Check the belt size, the wheel size and the guard are sized to one another, and the guard that is fitted for the belt size stays on every time.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction and the right respiratory protection for the dust load; the U.S. PPE baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the setup side is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grain, the size, the backing and the bond in the enquiry, so a silicon carbide abrasive belt order does not arrive as the wrong belt; the scale reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).
- Check the inspection report and any certification you rely on come with the order, because the record the team will need on the day is the one on the paperwork.
- Confirm the storage and the transport, and the packing that keeps the belt's splice from stepping is the one to check before it leaves the store.
- Follow the belt maker's install notes for the machine, and the splice that is walked flat is the one that does not mark the work.
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any bulk or [OEM private-label order](/oem), and start with the [quotation form](/quote).
