### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistake. Here is what shows up most often, with the fix that actually moves the problem:

- **Skipping grits.** Going from P120 straight to P320 leaves P120 scratches under the finish; add the P180 step and the surface clears, and the finish sits evenly over it.
- **Pressing into the pad.** The harder the lean, the more the face loads and the less it cuts; lighter pressure and a moving pad cut more than heavy pressure ever will.
- **Reusing a worn PSA face.** A face that has lifted at the edge or thinned out cuts uneven no matter the grit; pull the pad at the first sign of lift and match the abrasive to the mandrel, not to habit.
- **Storing pads damp or hot.** A pad kept in a damp bay or against a heat source can delaminate or warp before it ever cuts; store them dry, cool and away from direct sun, and check the face for warp before the run.
- **Mixing grit scales between suppliers.** Two shops, two scales, two "P240" pads that are not the same grain size; keep the shop to one scale and name it on every order, and the mismatch does not happen on the bench.

For any of these, the grit and backing to reach for are in [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs), the terms that keep the diagnosis straight are at [the abrasives glossary](/en/glossary), and the machine pairing that sets the backing is in the section above. The single thread through all five is that the pad is only as good as the backing, the tool and the grit you matched it with before the job started; most of the complaints are really mismatch complaints wearing a grit's name.


### FAQ

**Which backing should I buy for a random orbital sander?** In many cases hook-and-loop, because the two hold fast and the orbit keeps the pad face from clogging; if the orbital also takes a vacuum, a PSA pad is the pick. The pairing is set out in [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs), and the 5-inch size is the standard fit at [the 5-inch PSA sanding disc](/en/products/5-inch-psa-sanding-disc).

**When is 320 grit the right step?** Usually as the final prep before a finish, once P180 or P240 has done the leveling; on panels and doors it is often the last abrasive pass before stain or lacquer, and it is the stage where the backing quality shows up most. The mesh pad is the shape that suits it, visible at [the 5-inch mesh sanding discs](/en/products/5-inch-mesh-sanding-disc).

**Can I use a solid pad on a random orbital?** No; a solid pad is built for a belt finishing machine that carries it in one body and re-sharpens it. On an orbital the pad will not seat and will cut nothing; match the shape to the machine first, then pick the grit. The solid line is at [the 6-inch non-hole sanding disc](/en/products/6-inch-solid-sanding-disc).

**What does a "dustless" claim on a pad spec actually mean?** It means the pad face sheds dust into the gaps and does not hold it against the work the way a sealed face would; it does not mean no dust is produced. Read it as "less loaded on the work" and keep the extractor and the mask on anyway.

**Do I need dust extraction for finishing work?** In many cases yes, and the spec should follow the material: a fine, hard dust needs a different filter and mask than a coarse, soft one. The control that carries the whole thing is dust extraction at the point of work, and the setup reading is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).

**How do I buy sanding pads in bulk or on a private label run?** Send the six spec-sheet fields — grain, grit on a named scale, backing and shape, tool and rated speed, bond, and label or packing — and the order is a conversation, not a gamble; start at [request a quotation](/en/quote) and the branded side is on [the OEM and private label service page](/en/oem).

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name the grit scale — FEPA or ISO 848 — plus the backing and the diameter; that keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale and a different pad. The scale is set out at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and the standards body behind it is at [ISO standards](https://www.iso.org/).

**Why do my pads die early, and how long should they last?** A pad life is set by the tool and the backing, not the grit: a hook-and-loop pad on an orbital wears evenly, a PSA pad dies when the face lifts at the edge, and a solid pad life is always against the named machine. If they are dying early and the face is not the worn part, you are on the wrong backing for the tool; the machine pairing and the grit to match are in [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs).


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's rated no-load speed is at or below what the pad is rated for before every run; if the speed is not printed, confirm the rated speed with sales and keep the tool below it.
- Match the backing to the machine: hook-and-loop to a random orbital, PSA to a vacuum or orbital mandrel, solid to a belt finishing machine, mesh to a light finishing pass.
- Seat the pad fully on the mandrel and check the grip or glue-down before it touches the work; a loose or lifting face cuts uneven, and the guard or platen is the right size for the pad every time.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction and a respiratory protection sized to the dust load, not to the habit.
- State the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the backing and the diameter in every order so you do not mix scales or shapes between suppliers.
- Ask for the certificate or inspection report on the lot, and for any private label run, confirm the label, the packing and the spec sheet on the box before the order is cut.
- Store pads dry and cool, away from direct sun and heat, and check the face for warp or lift before the run; follow the maker's install notes on the mandrel or the belt, not the habit.
- For a bulk or branded run, confirm lead time, minimum order and the packing spec, and start with [the quotation form](/en/quote) so the grit, the backing and the box all match the sheet you sent.

Keep a record of the PPE and controls you rely on; the U.S. PPE baseline is at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the setup reading is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels), and the scale you buy to is set out at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).
