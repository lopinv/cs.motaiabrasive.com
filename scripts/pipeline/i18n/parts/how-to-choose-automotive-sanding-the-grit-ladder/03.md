### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistake, and each one is a match problem rather than a bad-disc problem. Here is what shows up most often on automotive work, with the fix that actually moves the issue:

- **Skipping a rung** ⚠️ — going from P80 to P180 without P150 leaves the P80 scratches under the primer and the top coat. The fix is the rung you skipped, added back in, not a coarser grit on the next panel.
- **Loading and glazing** ⚠️ — the face packs with soft filler or the grain glazes flat and the cut drops. The fix is an open weave or a harder grain, a step to a finer grit, and keeping the disc moving rather than pressing into the panel.
- **Edge burn** ⚠️ — a fine grit pressed into a thin panel edge takes a dent or a heat mark into the finish later. The fix is the finer side of the grit for the edge and a moving disc, not more pressure.
- **Grit-scale mixup** ⚠️ — a P150 on one scale and a bare 150 on another are not always the same grain, and the two discs cut differently on the same panel. The fix is naming FEPA or ISO 848 in every order and keeping the shop on one scale; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).
- **A worn hook side on the pad** ⚠️ — a PSA disc that feels like it is slipping mid-pass is usually a pad that has lost its grip, not a disc that is spent. The fix is a new pad, not a new set of discs.
- **Running the tool over the disc's limit** ⚠️ — a disc fed past its printed maximum working speed is a failure waiting for a panel. The fix is a smaller disc on the same tool, a slower tool on the same disc, or confirming the rated speed with sales so the two numbers are known and matched.

For any of these, [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) is where the grit and the format to reach for are visible, [the automotive repair sanding use](/en/applications/automotive) notes carry the sequence by job, and [the abrasives glossary](/en/glossary) keeps the terms straight so you and your team are diagnosing the same problem. The thread through all six is that the disc is as good as the grit, the format and the speed it was matched to before the job started, and most of the complaints are match complaints wearing a grit's name.


### FAQ

**What grits should I budget for on a typical automotive sanding job?** A degloss and refinish runs P320 to P400, then P180 to P220 on the filler and primer, and a wet finish runs P800 to P1500. A full re-spray of a panel adds the coarse rungs, P80 to P150, under the filler. The sequence is always coarse to fine, one rung at a time.

**Can I skip a grit rung on a panel?** No, not if the finish will be checked under light. A skipped rung leaves the coarser scratch under the next coat, and that is the grit-line failure the whole ladder exists to avoid. If a rung is missing, the fix is to add it back, not to close the gap with pressure.

**What grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA, the ISO 848 scale, is the one to name, because it is the scale our range is built on and the one where P180 on your order and P180 on our label are the same grain. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**Hook and loop or mesh, which for a paint shop?** Hook and loop for the degloss and finishing work, where the swap speed and the cushion matter, and mesh for the long, continuous passes where clogging would otherwise stop the cut. The two are the formats a paint shop keeps on the pad and on the shelf, and the decision is the format-to-job list above.

**Is a 2400 disc worth keeping on the shelf?** For the brightening rung, yes in most finishing workflows: a 2400 disc takes the P1500 scratches out and leaves a surface the compound and polish can actually bring to a finish. It is not a removal grit, so it is the disc you run last, and the format to use it in is the fine, open one.

**How many discs per panel should I expect?** It moves with the grit and the format, and the honest answer is that a coarse PSA disc gets used up on one heavy filler panel while a fine mesh disc carries many, so the number to manage is the cost per finished panel, not the count. Confirm the face life for your grit against your panel with sales, and keep your own panel count running, because the two numbers together are what the budget is built on.

**What safety gear is non-negotiable on the shop floor?** Eye and face protection on every pass, a mask or respirator rated for the dust load, and a dust extractor at the point of work. The PPE baseline is at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the tool-setup side is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels). The risk on these tools is reduced under compliant use, not removed, so the gear is the part of the job that is not optional.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist, a reminder rather than a substitute for your own site rules, and the underlining is that under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed before every use, and confirm the rated speed with sales where the disc does not list one.
- Check the arbor, pad or backing fit and any guard the tool carries, and use a rated adapter where the hole does not match.
- Wear eye and face protection, and a mask or respirator rated for the dust load, with a dust extractor at the point of work.
- Run dust extraction and keep a waste bin that is not a swept corner, because the fine grits throw more dust, not less.
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, plus the grit, the format and the diameter, in every order so two suppliers do not ship two different grains under the same number.
- Check the disc for delamination, cracking or a worn face before it goes on the pad, and keep the hook side on your pad in the same shape.
- Confirm the certification or inspection report for the run you are ordering, and keep the label's speed and arbor numbers where the operator can see them.
- Store the discs flat, dry and away from heat, and follow the maker's install and use notes for the format you are running.
- For a bulk or branded run, confirm the minimum order quantity and the lead time with sales, and start with the [request a quotation](/en/quote) page.
