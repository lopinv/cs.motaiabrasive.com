### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on raw wood?** In many cases P80; on heavy old paint or rough sawn stock, start at P60. Then step 80 to 120 to 240, and if the spec asks for a silkier face than P240 leaves, add the 800 step on a light pass. Never jump two steps at a time.

**Which width should a bench sander run?** For most bench belt sanders, the 610 × 100 mm loop is the size the machine is built around, and the 533 × 75 mm loop covers the light touch-up work. If you move to a floor machine, the wide sanding belt in 1520 × 630 or 2200 × 1330 is the class you are in, and the rated line speed is the number to confirm first.

**Belt or disc on a curved surface?** The disc. A platen wants flat, open work; a pad follows a curve, a recess and a small part. Curved or detailed work stays on the [hook-and-loop sanding disc](/en/products/hook-loop-sanding-discs) side of the bench.

**What does "maximum line speed" mean, and where do I find it?** It is the fastest the loop is allowed to run at the surface, in metres per minute, and it comes from the belt's spec — confirm it against your machine's no-load speed at the wheel size you use, and keep the machine below it. We confirm the rated speed for each model with sales before an order ships.

**Can one belt do both the stock removal and the finish?** No. One grit does one job. A P80 loop leaves a P80 cut; the finish wants the P120 to P240 bands behind it. Two grits minimum, each finer than the last, is the sequence that saves the surface.

**Which grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, stated next to the grit in every line of the order, so two suppliers cannot quietly ship you two different cuts. The scale itself is explained at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and the [abrasives glossary](/en/glossary) is where to keep the terms straight.

**How do I buy industrial sanding belts in volume?** Pick the one size your machine runs daily and the grit band the line uses, send the grit, the width, the grain and the grit standard to an abrasive belt supplier, and confirm MOQ, lead time and the inspection report before the order. For a branded or OEM run, start at [the OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem) and put the order in through [request a quotation](/en/quote).


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. It is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these machines is reduced, not removed.

- Confirm your machine's no-load speed at the wheel size you use is below the belt's rated maximum line speed, before every run.
- Confirm the belt width and loop length fit the machine, the drive wheels are in spec, and the platen guard is on.
- Wear eye and face protection on every pass, and match the respiratory protection to the dust load the grit and the material put up.
- Run dust extraction at the point of generation, with a filter for the material, and keep the waste in a bin, not on the floor.
- Name the grit standard — FEPA or ISO 848 — next to every grit in the enquiry, so the order cannot be mixed with a different scale.
- Ask for the lot inspection report and confirm which certifications your market requires from an abrasive belt supplier; check them against [the manufacturing & quality page](/en/factory) before the order.
- Store loops flat, dry and out of the sun, and transport them without kinks.
- Follow the install notes: set the tension, check the wheel alignment, and confirm the splice style suits the grit and the load.
