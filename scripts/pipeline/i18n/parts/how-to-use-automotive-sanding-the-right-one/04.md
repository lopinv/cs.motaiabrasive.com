### Before you buy ✅

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. It is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's no-load speed is below the disc's rated maximum speed before every use; the rating comes from the disc, not from a guess.
- Check the pad and the disc format match — hook-and-loop pad to hook-and-loop disc, PSA to PSA, mesh to mesh — and the pad size to the disc size.
- Fit and keep the tool guard and pad true; a loose or wobbly pad is a wavy mark before it is anything else.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run respiratory protection matched to the dust class for the coating system you are on.
- Keep dust extraction running at the work, and give the waste a bin; wet-sanding streams are their own bin.
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, in every order so the scales do not mix.
- Check the certs and the inspection report for the grade you are buying; the [manufacturing and quality page](/factory) is where ours are described, and the rest is confirmed with sales.
- Store the discs flat, dry and away from heat and sun, and keep the pad faces clean; a stored pad face is the same worn part it was on the bench.
- Follow the install notes on the disc and the pad pair, and re-check the fit each shift.
- For U.S. baselines, the PPE side is [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the tool side is [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.244](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.244); a plain-language read of the setup is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
