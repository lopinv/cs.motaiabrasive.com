### Common mistakes and the fixes that actually move the problem

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistake. Here is what shows up most on the floor, with the fix that actually moves the problem:

- **Over-pressing a fine grit** 🚫 — pressing into a P400 or P600 disc makes it glaze, which is the opposite of cutting more. Lighter pressure and a moving pad, not a coarser grit, is the fix.
- **Skipping grits** ⏭️ — P80 straight to P400 leaves P80 scratches under the finish; add the P120 and P240 steps and the surface clears.
- **Edge peel from a dirty pad** 💥 — a loop side packed with dust or wet from a wash lets the disc skid and peel at the edge. Clean the pad, seat the disc fully, and the peel stops.
- **Wrong tool speed** ⏱️ — a disc running past its rated line speed glazes and flexes; lower the tool speed or run a smaller pad, and never push the disc past the printed maximum.
- **Worn face doing the last pass** 🧽 — a disc that has been sanded past its grit face will not finish; replace the fine grit rather than chase a dead face, because the fine grits are the consumable.
- **Embedded steel on soft parts** 🧊 — running a disc that has touched mild steel onto stainless or a non-ferrous part seeds rust spots later; keep a dedicated disc per material, or swap grits between materials.
- **Belt-work contamination** ⚙️ — a surface that has been belt-sanded and then turned to a disc still carries the belt's scratch angle; add the blending grits (P120, P240) before the finish line, or the belt marks show through.

For any of these, the [sandpaper grit standards page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA) is the reference for the scale, and the [abrasives glossary](/en/glossary) keeps the terms straight so you and your team are diagnosing the same problem. The thread that runs through all seven is that most of the complaints are really match complaints wearing a grit's name: fix the grit, the grain, the backing or the speed, and the cut comes back.


### FAQ

**What grit do I start with for general sanding?** In many cases P80 to P120 covers everyday removal and blending on most substrates, then step down to P240 or P400 for the finish. Start coarse, work down, and never jump two steps at a time.

**What does hook and loop actually give me over a taped paper disc?** Seconds to swap grits, a clean discard, and no curled paper strips, so keeping a bench of P80, P240 and P600 becomes practical. The trade is that the bond needs a clean pad side, or the disc will edge-peel.

**What is the P600 step for?** The 600 grit is the leveling step: it reduces texture after putty or 2K primer and closes the surface ahead of a clear coat. It is not a removal grit; on bare or heavily textured stock, P80 to P240 does that job first.

**Can I run a 6-inch disc on the same tool as a 4-inch disc?** Only if the pad fits the disc diameter and the tool's RPM keeps the disc below its rated line speed. A larger pad turns at the same RPM but reaches a higher line speed, so confirm the rated speed against your model before you swap sizes.

**Why does my disc stop cutting?** Usually glazing from over-pressing or running too fast, or a worn face on a fine grit. Lighter pressure and a moving pad bring the cut back; on a fine grit that has been sanded past its face, replace the disc instead.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 grit plus the diameter and arbor, so the order is not mixed against a different grit scale. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and that is the standard every grit number in this piece is quoted on.

**When is an abrasive belt the better tool than a disc?** A belt wins on stock removal, long straight edges and consistent feed on a sander; a hook-and-loop disc wins on the finish line and on changing grit fast. ### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed rated maximum working speed before every use; when in doubt, confirm the rated speed against your specific model with sales.
- Check the pad diameter and the disc diameter match, and that the pad's loop side is clean and in shape, or the disc will edge-peel.
- Wear eye and face protection on every pass, and use dust extraction and the right respiratory protection for the dust load, especially on the fine grits.
- Confirm the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every order, so the P600 you order is the P600 you get.
- Ask for the inspection report or certificate for a bulk or branded run, and check the storage and transport notes so the discs are not damp or crushed on arrival.
- Follow the disc maker's install notes, and check the part for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material.
- Keep a record of the PPE and controls you rely on; the U.S. baseline is [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and setup guidance is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any [sanding discs wholesale](/en/collections/sanding-discs) or [private-label order](/en/oem), and start with the [quotation form](/en/quote).
