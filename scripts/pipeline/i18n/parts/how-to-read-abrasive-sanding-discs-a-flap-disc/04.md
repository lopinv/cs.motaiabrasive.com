### FAQ

**What grit do I start with for general prep work?** In many cases P80 to P120 for removal and P180 to P220 for an even, coat-ready surface. Start coarse, work down one rung at a time, and let the finish you are aiming for pick the last grit.

**What is the difference between PSA and hook-and-loop discs?** A PSA disc sticks to a bare mandrel with an adhesive film; a hook-and-loop disc clips onto a loop-covered mandrel and swaps in seconds. Keep one type per tool, and match the type to the mandrel you actually have.

**Are perforated discs worth it on an orbital sander?** They are the natural match: the holes vent dust through the face instead of letting it seal in, which keeps the cut open on dusty work. On a low-dust, fine-grit job the slightly smaller cutting face can show, so a standard PSA disc is fine there.

**When do I reach for a 2400 grit?** A 2400-grit pass is for a surface that is already near final: cutting the very last fine scratches out of a cured coat, or polishing the marks a P1500 left. It is the last grit in the sequence, not the one that starts it.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848, plus the grit, the diameter, the mounting type and the quantity. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and naming the scale keeps your order from being matched against a disc on a different scale.

**How do I buy abrasive sanding discs in bulk?** Send grain, grit, standard, diameter, mounting and quantity to a supplier, and ask for the wear rate and lead time by grit. The [request a quotation](/quote) page is where a bulk or branded order starts, and the [OEM & private-label service page](/oem) covers what a branded run can carry on the box.

**Why does my disc clog and stop cutting?** Loading or glazing: the material is packing the coat, or the grit has sealed flat. Lighter pressure, a moving disc, a tougher grain, or a coarser grit that sheds the material. Clean the face where you can, and if the disc keeps sealing, the grain is too soft for the workpiece.

**What does a dustless setup actually change?** It moves the dust from the operator's breath to a filter: extraction at the tool face, the extractor running at the work, and waste that ends in a bag instead of a corner. Under compliant controls the exposure is reduced, not removed, which is why eye and face protection and the right respiratory gear stay in the loop.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant speed, correct fit and PPE the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed before every use, and keep the tool below it.
- Check the mandrel or arbor fit and the disc type: a PSA disc on a bare mandrel, a hook-and-loop disc on a loop mandrel, and for flap discs the correct arbor hole with a rated adapter where needed.
- Keep the guard fitted on any grinding tool at all times; it is the first defence against a disc that fails.
- Wear eye and face protection on every pass, and use the right respiratory protection for the dust load, with dust extraction running at the work.
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, in every abrasive sanding discs order, with grain, grit, diameter, mounting and quantity on the same line.
- Ask for the inspection report or certificate of conformity for the run, and keep it with the order record.
- Store discs away from heat and moisture and keep them in their packaging until use; a grain that is bruised or damp before the job is a disc that will not run as quoted.
- Follow the maker's install notes for the type you bought, and on thin or soft material check the part for heat and scratch depth as you go.
- Plan the dust side with the grit: coarse removal on a dusty substrate wants extraction set up before the first pass, not after the complaint.
