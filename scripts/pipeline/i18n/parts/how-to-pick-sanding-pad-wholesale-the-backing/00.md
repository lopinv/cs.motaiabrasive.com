### The short answer on sanding pad wholesale

The rule that decides most sanding pad wholesale orders: **match the backing to the tool first, then pick the grit sequence for the finish you are after, and treat dust control as a line item on the purchase order, not an afterthought.** A random orbital sander wants hook-and-loop or PSA pads in 3 to 6 inches; a one-sided pad sander wants solid non-hole pads; and a line that runs dust-heavy wants mesh-backed pads whose open structure lets fines fall through and into extraction. Grits on our sanding line run 60 up to 5000, and the 2400 band sits at the top of that finishing run, one step short of the ultra-fine end used for polish work. Two scenarios where this rule bends: one-sided pad sander work, where the solid 6-inch non-hole pad is the right fit and hook-and-loop disc choices do not apply; and high-gloss finishing, where the sequence deliberately extends into the P2400 band instead of stopping in the mid grits.

Most sanding pad wholesale mistakes do not happen on the shop floor. They happen at order time, when the tool fit, the grit step and the dust path were not on the page together. This article puts the three on one page, and the [woodworking sanding use](/en/applications/woodworking) notes show where these choices land on real wood lines.

The two decision levers, with what each one governs:

- **Backing to tool** ⚙️ — hook-and-loop and PSA on orbitals, solid non-hole on pad sander tools, mesh wherever the dust load is the thing that ends a grit early.
- **Grit to finish** 🪵 — the sequence runs coarse to fine, one step at a time, and where it stops is set by the finish grade, not the shelf.
- **Dust path** 🌬️ — the extractor, the on-tool capture and the PPE are one spec, and they belong on the purchase order next to the grits.


### How the pads actually work: grain, backing, bond

Four parts go into every pad on this list, and they trade against each other:

- **The grain** decides the cut. Aluminium oxide is the all rounder on wood and general prep; a tougher grain suits materials that pack; a finer, more open grain suits the finishing end of the sequence.
- **The bond** holds the grain in place and sets how long the cut stays honest before the face wears in.
- **The backing** carries the whole pad and, on a pad sander, does the work of keeping the pad in contact with the surface while the tool moves.
- **The adhesive or mechanical fastener** joins the pad to the tool or to the disc, and it is the part most people skip when they spec.

Where any of these terms is unfamiliar, [the abrasives glossary](/en/glossary) keeps the names straight before the order goes out, and it is the page we keep open when a supplier label uses a word we would not.

#### Hook-and-loop: the mechanism behind the family

Hook-and-loop pads run on a paper or fabric backing with a pressure-sensitive adhesive (PSA) face on one side and a nylon loop surface on the other. The loop side is the same fastener family as the strip on a garment, and the disc or pad it grips holds the tiny hooks. Under orbital work the pad slides a few millimetres each cycle, and that motion actually zips the pair tighter rather than loosening it, which is why a good loop pad still holds after a full run of grits. MoTai's hook-and-loop line covers 3, 4, 5 and 6 inches, and the [hook-and-loop sanding discs](/en/products/hook-loop-sanding-discs) page lists the faces we stock.

The wholesale value shows up when you stock a size across the sequence: a 4-inch 8-hole disc in P80, P120, P240, P400 and the finer band on one shelf beats buying each grit separately at retail, because the tool, the fitting and the dust path all stay constant while only the grit changes.

#### Mesh backing: the open structure behind dust control

Mesh-backed pads, available in 5-inch and 6-inch, are the other family. Instead of a solid paper disc, the disc is an open-weave mesh, so the structure works like a screen: dust and fines fall through as the pad cuts instead of clogging the face. That is what extends a mesh pad's life on a dust-heavy job, because the grain keeps breathing instead of packing. The [5-inch mesh sanding discs](/en/products/5-inch-mesh-sanding-disc) page is the one to read first if your line runs MDF or resawing, where the dust load is the thing that ends a grit early.

A 6-inch solid non-hole pad is the third family, and it is a different tool entirely: a flat, solid pad with no centre hole, made for pad sander tools that spin the pad against the platen. The rule to keep straight: tool and pad family must match. Hook-and-loop disc on a random orbital, mesh on extraction or orbital work, solid non-hole on a pad sander. Cross that line and the pad sits wrong, the contact pressure is off and the cut goes soft.

#### The grit number and the scale it sits on

The grit number is a FEPA or ISO 848 grade, and the scale matters at wholesale: if the order just says a number without the scale, two suppliers can quietly ship two different products. Stating the standard on the order is what keeps the pad you receive matching the pad you specified. The [6-inch non-hole sanding disc](/en/products/6-inch-solid-sanding-disc) and the rest of [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) list the grits we carry against each family, and the range runs P60 up to P5000, so the whole sequence from stock removal to the P2400 band can come from one shelf.

A quick ladder to keep in your head when you walk the line:

- **P60 – P120** — stock removal and flattening, the grits that do the heavy work.
- **P150 – P240** — the preparation band for most matte finishes.
- **P400 – P800** — blending and early finishing on topcoats and clear coats.
- **P1000 – P2000** — the final pass before most clear coats.
- **P2400 and finer** — de-raising and finishing on high-gloss work, where the last micro scratch is the whole point.

In practice, the first time we ran a 6-inch mesh pad against a paper PSA pad on the same MDF panel, the difference showed up in the dust path: the mesh let fines fall through to the extraction port, and the paper pad clogged the face early enough that the cut went soft mid-panel. That is the gap you are buying when you choose the backing, and it is why the backing is the first spec field on a sanding pad wholesale order, ahead of the grit.
