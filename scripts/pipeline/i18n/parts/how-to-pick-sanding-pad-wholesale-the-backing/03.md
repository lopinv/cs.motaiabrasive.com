### FAQ

**What grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, stated on the order, so that a P240 means the same thing on both sides of the transaction. The scale is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA) and the full definition sits with [ISO standards](https://www.iso.org/). Naming the standard is the step that keeps your P2400 pads matching your P2400 pads when you reorder from a second source. The terms are defined at [the abrasives glossary](/en/glossary).

**Does "dustless" mean no dust?** No. Dustless pads mean on-tool dust capture, the mesh or the ported disc feeding the extractor at the source, which means less airborne dust to handle. It does not make the airborne dust go away on its own, so you still run extraction and keep PPE on for the load that stays airborne. The OSHA PPE baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132).

**When do I actually need 2400 grit pads?** High-gloss and reflective finishes, where the topcoat reflects the scratch direction it sits on, and visible door faces where the last micro scratch is the whole problem. Matte lacquer is usually done at P1500 or P2000, and the P2400 band is where you stop.

**How do I sequence grits without trapping scratches?** One step at a time, each grit removing roughly half the scratch width of the one before it, and counting steps down from what the panel already has rather than up from what the shelf offers. The worked sequence in the material section is the template to follow.

**Can I mix pad types on the same sander?** Hook-and-loop sticks to hook-and-loop faces; mesh and PSA are separate families and do not grip each other. A mesh disc will not stick to a PSA pad, and a solid non-hole pad needs a pad sander tool, not an orbital. Swap the pads when you change the tool type, and keep the families separated on the shelf so a pad does not stay stuck under the next one.

**What do I need for a wholesale or bulk order?** State the grits, the backing family, the size and the grit standard per item, and the quantity per item, on the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form. For a branded run, add the label and the pack spec, and confirm the MOQ and the lead time with sales on each item. The full structure is on the [OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem).

**Which grain do I match to my material?** Aluminium oxide is the default all rounder on wood and most prep work; a tougher grain suits materials that pack; and the finishing band usually runs on a softer, more open grain for the even cut. The grain options on the [sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) cover all four, and the grain to material match is a call to confirm with sales against your own panels.

**What changes when the supplier switches the grain or the bond?** The grit number on the pad is not the whole spec, because the grain and the bond decide how the cut feels and how long the face lasts. If a supplier swaps the grain or the bond between orders, the pad can cut differently and last differently on the same grit, and the move to flag is the pad face against a panel, not the label. Confirm the grain and the bond on the spec, and ask the supplier to note any change on the next order, because a grit that cuts differently on the same label is the one that re-trains the whole line.

**Which grit band spends the most per finished panel?** The P2400 band and the grits just above it, because the face goes fastest on the finer grits and the panels are where the finish grade is set. If the finish is matte, the P2400 band is not in the sequence and the cost question does not come up; if the finish is high-gloss, the P2400 band is the one to stock and the one to plan the line around, because it is the grit the next panel needs to finish.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not removed.

- Confirm your tool's no-load speed is at or below the pad's rated maximum speed for the model you are ordering; check the pad's rated speed against the tool before the first panel.
- Check the pad diameter and the mounting fit against the tool, and the tool's guard or dust port where it has one.
- Fit and keep eye and face protection at the tool, and keep respiratory protection on for the dust load; the U.S. baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the setup reading is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Run dust extraction at the work point, and fit on-tool capture where the pad feeds it, so the dust path is designed in rather than swept up after.
- State the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grit band, the backing family and the size in every order, so the pad you receive matches the pad you specified.
- Check the inspection or quality report on any private-label run, and confirm the label and the pack against the spec before the order ships.
- Store pads dry, warm, flat and out of direct sun, stacked as packed, so the adhesive bond does not weaken before the job.
- Follow the pad maker's installation notes on backing and on pad replacement, and swap the pad family when the tool type changes.
