### Q5: which belt for the volume line, and the widths to build from

The belt is where the volume line shows up, and the width is the field that follows the face, because the machine takes the belt and the board takes the face. The widths to build from are the four in the range:

- **The 533 by 75 and the 610 by 100** — the bench sander's widths, and the one the detail and the medium board runs on. The [610 × 100 mm abrasive belt](/products/610x100-abrasive-belt) is the standard on the bench, and the grit is the same ladder as the wide machine so the two tools cut the same way.
- **The 1520 by 630 and the 2200 by 1330** — the wide machine's widths, and the one the volume board runs on. The Wide Belt is the tool for the face that goes through the line, and the belt is the form that keeps the cut even across the board.

The rule is that the width follows the face and the grit follows the board, and the grit is the same ladder across the two widths so the surface does not show the tool change. The Abrasive Belts family on the wide machine is the volume answer, and the disc on the orbital is the detail answer, and the two forms sharing the same grit is the split that keeps the board honest. The [abrasive belt range](/collections/abrasive-belts) shows the widths and the grits to pair, and the machine and the belt have to agree on the width before the order ships, because the two that do not agree are the two that show on the surface instead of the spec.


### Q6: what is the cost number, and how the sample earns the bulk

The cost number on a Wood Sanding run is cost per board, not price per belt, and the run is where the trap bites hardest, because the stripping step is long, the board is the visible work and the cheap belt is the one that glazes on the gummy wood.

- **The belt that holds its cut** — the grain and the charge that run to the last pass without the face dulling, and the unit price is higher but the belts-per-board is lower, so the ratio is the number that wins on the long run.
- **The belt that glazes** — the face that dulls by the second board and the swap that is two a panel instead of one, and the unit price is lower but the belts-per-board is higher, so the ratio is the number that loses.

The match test is the scrap board: take a piece of the job wood, run the candidate belt through the sequence, count the belts, and divide by the boards finished. Do it against the incumbent and the two come back as one ratio, and that ratio is the number that belongs in the order conversation, not the per-belt price. The bulk follows the sample, and the sample runs on the job wood, because the comparison that matters is the surface after, not the box in hand. One habit from the floor: keep the scrap board and the pass count the same for every candidate, and the [quotation form](/quote) is where the number gets built, because the two that survive the scrap board are the two the bulk earns. A Wood Sanding run that skips the sample is the run that ships the cheap belt into the line, and the fault shows up on the board a week after the shelf is full, and the fix is the sample that was not run.


### Q7: what to send in the enquiry, and the fields that make it

The fields are the order, and the supplier who answers all of them is the one to standardize on. The six, in the order the supplier reads them:

- **Grit and the standard** — the grit, and the FEPA or ISO 848 scale on the same one the shop buys to.
- **Grain and the charge** — the grain, and the charge when comparing two belts that both say the same grit.
- **Form and the width** — the disc, the belt or the sheet, and the width on the belt, chosen to the tool and the face as above.
- **Quantity and lead time** — the order quantity and the shelf date, which sets whether it ships from stock or gets made to the run.
- **Label and packing** — the logo, the part code and the spec sheet on the box for a branded run.
- **The finish line** — the spec the sequence ends at, because the grit the next trade wants is the grit the order builds to.

Send those fields and the order is a conversation instead of a gamble. The [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) walks the branded run, and the [quotation form](/quote) takes the fields so the order starts with the ones that make or break it. One note on the numbers we share: we list the grit, the form, the grain and the standard as standard, and we confirm the rated speed against each model before an order ships, because that number has to come from the belt, not from a guess. The six fields are the Wood Sanding order, and the supplier who builds the belt to all six is the one the line standardizes on, because the one who builds to the price is the one the line outgrows by the second run.
