### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on a plastic backing flap disc?** In many cases P80 to P120 for the last of the removal, then P220 to P400 for the finish. If the job still has real material coming off, that stage belongs on a steel face, and the plastic takes over once the surface is near its finish line.

**What is the difference between plastic and fiberglass backings?** Plastic is lighter and cooler for general finishing, a fiberglass backing resists flex and heat better and carries the high-speed jobs, and steel is the rigid face for deburring and cap removal. Pick by how hard the job is and how fast the tool runs, and confirm the choice on [the fiberglass-backing flap disc](/en/flap/fiberglass) and [the steel-backing flap disc](/en/flap/steel) pages.

**Why does my P220 disc glaze on castings?** The cast skin packs the grain, so the face loads and stops cutting. Step the grit down a rung and move to zirconia alumina, which is the open-cutting grain for materials that pack aluminium oxide, and keep the disc moving rather than pressing into the part.

**Can I run the same plastic disc on a 125 mm and a 180 mm grinder?** Only if the no-load speed on each tool stays under the disc's printed maximum for that diameter, because surface speed rises with both diameter and RPM. When in doubt, measure the tool at no load and keep the disc below its label, and confirm the rated speed against the model.

**Does a plastic backing cut slower than steel on the same grit?** Yes, the flexible face skims where the rigid face drives, so for the same grit the plastic disc removes less per pass. That is the trade you are buying: a cooler, more even finish in exchange for the raw removal rate, and it is why the heavy stage of a sequence stays on a steel face.

**How do I buy plastic backing flap discs in bulk or under my own label?** Send the grit, the backing, the diameter, the arbor and the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) to the supplier, and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form and the [OEM and private-label service page](/en/oem) are where a bulk or branded order starts, with the minimum order quantity and lead time confirmed against your run.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 grit, plus the diameter and the arbor; that keeps the order from being mixed with a different grit scale from another supplier. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and the ISO index is at [ISO standards](https://www.iso.org/).

**When does P220 go out of its job?** When the surface is already in the P320 band, or when the operator has been pressing into one spot and the face glazed; the fix is P320 to P400 for the cleanup stage, or lighter pressure with the disc moving, not a coarser grit.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- ✅ Confirm your tool's maximum no-load speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed for that diameter, before the first pass, and confirm the rated speed against the model.
- 🔩 Check the arbor hole and the flange are the correct size, and use a rated adapter where the hole does not match, never a filed or ground larger hole.
- 🛡️ Fit and keep the tool guard on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc that fails at speed.
- 🧊 Pick the grit band by the finish line, name the standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in the enquiry, and confirm the plastic backing is the right one for the speed and the material.
- 🌫️ Set up dust extraction at the work with the right filter for the material, and the respiratory protection that matches the load, with the PPE baseline at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the setup guidance at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- 📦 Follow the maker's install notes for the flange and the seating, and check the part for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material before the next stage.
- 🗃️ Store the discs dry and off the floor, out of the sun, and keep the labels legible, because the grit, the backing and the speed all have to be readable when the disc goes on the tool.
- 🚚 Confirm lead time, minimum order quantity and packing for any [flap disc range](/en/collections/flap-discs) bulk order or [private label run](/en/oem), and start with the [quotation form](/en/quote).
