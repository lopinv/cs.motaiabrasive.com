### FAQ

**How many grits should I keep on the bench for general work?** In many cases three cover a shop: a P120 or P180 for removal, a P320 to P400 for blend and prep, and a P1500 to P1800 for the fine end. Add a flap disc or two where the grinder does the heavy rungs, and the whole range is three rungs plus a tool, which is the amount of stock a running job actually touches.

**Can I buy the same grit number from two suppliers and expect the same cut?** No, not unless both are quoting the same standard. FEPA and the older ANSI scales do not line up number for number, so a P320 and a 320 grit can be different grain sizes on two different shelves. Name the standard in every enquiry and ask which scale the stock is on; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**When does a flap disc actually beat a sanding disc or a sheet?** When the job is removal, not finish. Paint strip, weld cap, deburring, and heavy blends run faster on a flap disc at the same grit, and the flaps reach the root of a weld a flat disc rides over. The last two rungs before a top coat are still the flat disc's job, because the even face is what the coat needs.

**What grit sequence for a fine wood finish?** P120 to take the unevenness off, P220 to P320 to even it up, P400 under the first coat, P800 to P1500 between coats, and 1800 to close it out before the top. Each coat raises the grain, so the sequence repeats after every coat that does it, and that repetition is what "raise and sand" means at the bench.

**What grit sequence for an automotive panel, primer to gloss?** P180 to block the primer, P320 to P400 to blend the sanded area into the skin, P800 to erase the P400 scratch, P1500 to P1800 to de-raise, and the coat. Start the fine rungs only where the surface is already clean; a P1500 over a P320 scratch is the cloudy-finish mistake, and it is the one that costs a repaint.

**What does an abrasive factory actually change on a bulk order?** Three things: the price has no chain margin on it, the spec is fixed so a P320 in spring and a P320 in autumn are the same cut, and the label and the packing can be yours. Confirm the minimum order, the lead time, and the inspection report that comes with the batch with sales before you quote a customer a price, because those three are what the quotation rests on.

**What PPE is the floor minimum for dry grinding and disc work?** Eye and face protection for everyone in the area, the respiratory protection that matches the dust load, and the extractor running at the work. The baseline documents are at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels), and compliant use with the right controls reduces the risk, not to zero.

**How do I start a private label run?** Send grit, backing, size, label, and quantity to [the quotation form](/quote), and ask for a sample against the spec before the full run so the grit, the backing, and the label all match before the order is committed. [The OEM & private-label service page](/oem) covers what goes on the packaging and the packing, and the [sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) is where the grit and the size start.


### Before you buy

This is a compliance and purchasing checklist, a reminder and not a substitute for your own site rules. In compliant use, with correct fit and the right protection, the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed before every use; where the label is missing, confirm the rated speed with sales and keep the tool below it.
- Check the arbor hole, the flange, and the guard are the correct size for the disc, use a rated adapter where needed, never file a hole larger, and keep the guard on.
- Eye and face protection at the work, and the respiratory protection that matches the dust load for the material, not the habit of the last job.
- Dust extraction actually running at the work, with a bin for the waste, and a step of extra control before the job starts where the material can carry silica.
- Name the grit standard in the enquiry, FEPA or ISO 848, with the grit, the backing, and the size on the same line so two scales do not meet on the bench.
- For a bulk order, check the certificates and the inspection report that come with the batch, and confirm what they cover with sales before the parts are committed to a customer.
- Store grits separated and dry, discs flat and uncrushed, and flanges and adapters out of the dust, because a crushed flap disc or a contaminated flange is a setup failure waiting to happen.
- Follow the maker's install notes on the disc, and check the part for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material before the next pass.
- For a wholesale or private label order, confirm the minimum order, the lead time, and the packing, and start with [the quotation form](/quote).
