### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on solid wood?** In many cases P80 to P100 on the heavier cloth for stock removal, then P150 or P180 to even, then P240 to P400 to prep the face before finish. Start coarse, work down, and never jump two steps at a time — a skipped grit is the scratch that shows through the finish.

**What is a 1800 grit belt actually for?** A 1800 is a de raise step, not a stock removal grit. It softens the scratch left by the P400 pass and leaves a face uniform enough for a clear coat or a raking light check. It sits at the very end of the sequence, on the top of the finish line, and not every belt size carries the fine end — confirm with sales that your machine holds that fine grit before you plan around it.

**Which belt size fits which machine?** 533 × 75 on the small bench and in line end, 610 × 100 on the everyday component machine, 1520 × 630 on mid range lines, and 2200 × 1330 on the wide belt panel machines. The size is set by the platen, and the rated line speed is set by the machine — send the make, model and platen width with the [request a quotation](/en/quote) and the team matches the belt to both.

**Can I run the same belt on hardwood and on MDF?** You can, and you will not get the same result from it. MDF loads a fine grit fast, so the same grit that preps a hardwood panel will glaze on the board. The fix is not a different belt on the same grit, it is a grit up on the board: the sequence runs coarser on MDF, lighter pressure, and the face is judged by the board's own finish target.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, plus the size, the grain, the cloth class and the machine you will run it on. The scale is a field on the spec sheet, not a footnote, and the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA). A shop that buys to two different scales ends up with two different grits under one number, and the face shows it.

**How do I buy abrasive belts in bulk, or with my own label?** Send the sizes, the grits, the grain, the cloth class, the loop length, your label and packing, and the quantity and the rate you need them on the shelf; the [OEM & private label service page](/en/oem) walks through what we can put on the box, and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form is where the order starts. Ask for a sample against the spec before the full run, so the grit, the class and the label all match what you expect.

**Does wood dust need its own extraction?** Yes, and the fine fraction is the part that matters — it stays airborne, not on the floor. Match the extraction to the material, run it at the source, and keep the team in rated protection where it does not reach; the PPE baseline is [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the setup reading is at [CCOHS "Abrasive Wheels"](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).

**How do I know if a belt is wearing out fast?** Look at the face, not the clock: heat marks, a scratch that is getting coarser mid pass, and a belt that stops cutting while the cloth still looks good are the wear signals. A short life across the whole sequence at once points at the machine — tracking, platen or pressure — before it points at the belts, and the cost per finished square metre is the number that settles which side is actually spending the money.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection, the risk of belt work is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm the machine's working line speed is below the belt's rated maximum before the belt goes on, and re check it whenever the platen or the motor changes.
- Check the platen and the belt width match, set the tracking, and keep every platen cover and guard fitted, on every pass.
- Wear eye and face protection, and the rated respiratory protection for the dust load, on every operator at the line.
- Run dust extraction at the source, sized to the material — the board dust is not the wood dust, so match them separately.
- Name the grit standard — FEPA or ISO 848 — the size, the grain, the cloth class and the loop form in every order, so a two supplier shelf does not mix two scales under one number.
- Ask for the inspection or test report that backs the line speed figure on the belt you are buying, and keep it with the machine file.
- Store belts flat, dry, out of direct heat, oldest stock to the machine first; a belt that aged in the store fails on the panel, not in the store.
- Follow the maker's install notes for the splice and the tension, and run each new belt in on the machine before it sees production stock.
- For a bulk or branded order, confirm the minimum order, the lead time and the label, and start the conversation with the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form, the sequences by wood at [woodworking sanding use](/en/applications/woodworking), and the full size set at [the abrasive belt range](/en/collections/abrasive-belts).
