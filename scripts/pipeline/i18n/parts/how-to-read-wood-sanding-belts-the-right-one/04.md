### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on a rough wood board?** In many cases P80 to P100 to take down machine marks and jointer lines, then P120 to P150 to even the surface, and P180 to P240 as the finishing pass before most clear coats. Start coarse, work down, and do not jump two steps at a time.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 for the grit, plus the belt size, the grain and the backing; that keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale or a different construction. The grit scale is the one the whole industry reads, and the reference for the grain side is at [the properties of aluminium oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_oxide).

**Can I run a 610 × 100 belt on the same machine as a 533 × 75 belt?** Only if the machine's drum and track are rated for the larger size, and if the machine speed at the larger size stays under the belt's printed maximum line speed. When in doubt, check the machine spec and the belt label before you fit it, and confirm with sales which sizes the model you run is stocked in.

**Why does my belt stop cutting?** Usually loading or glazing, most often on soft or sugary species, or a grit sequence that was skipped and left a coarser pattern under the fine belt. Step the grit, confirm the resin bond grade suits the removal pass, and keep the belt moving rather than pressing in.

**What does P2000 do that P400 does not?** P2000 is a pre-surface polish: it takes the very last marks off a sealed or filled surface before a clear coat that will show every fiber. P400 does the real prep; P2000 is the polish on top. You run P400 first and P2000 on a lightly sealed surface, not the other way around.

**How long does a wood sanding belt last?** It varies with the species, the pressure and the grit — a P80 on dense oak will run noticeably longer than a P80 on soft pine, because pine dust packs the grain sooner. Confirm the grain and the resin bond grade against the species you run, and ask the supplier for the expected life on your machine before you commit to a volume order.

**Can you put our logo on the belts?** Yes, that is the private label side of the range: your logo, your part code and any spec sheet on the box, with the minimum order quantity and lead time set by the run. Start at the [OEM & private-label service page](/oem), and send the size, grit, grain and backing you want branded through [request a quotation](/quote) so the branded run matches the stock you actually run.

**Which model should I send for a repeat order?** Send the model number, the grit and the size you want, and confirm the backing; the model carries the construction, the print and the speed, so ordering by model is the way to get the same belt back every time. [The GXK-51 abrasive belt](/model/gxk-51) and the rest of the model list are where to check the stock before you send the order.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these machines is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm the machine's maximum belt speed is below the belt's printed maximum line speed before every run; if the number is not printed, confirm the rated speed with the model and ask the supplier before you run it.
- Check the belt size against the machine's drum and track spec exactly; a belt cut short will not seat and a belt cut long will not seat, so the size has to match the spec, not the shelf.
- Fit and keep the machine guard and the tracking adjustment set before every run; the guard is the first defence against a belt that walks or separates.
- Wear eye protection, and use local dust extraction with a filter that suits the wood dust and respiratory protection for the load that the extractor does not catch.
- Confirm the grit, the grain, the belt size, the backing, the resin bond grade and the grit standard (ISO 848) in every order, so you do not mix scales or constructions between shipments.
- Check the certs and inspection report for the model you are ordering, and keep a record of the PPE and extraction you rely on; U.S. PPE requirements are a useful baseline at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and setup guidance is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Store belts flat or on a roll, away from heat and direct sun, and transport them so the seam does not catch on anything sharp; a damaged seam is a belt that will not seat.
- Follow the machine maker's install and tracking notes, and re-check the tracking and the belt condition at the start of every shift, not just at the start of the job.
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for a [volume or private label order](/oem), and start the conversation at the [quotation form](/quote).
