### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on a 6 inch disc?** In many cases P80 to P120 covers everyday sanding and material removal, then step down through P180 and P320 for blending and finish. Start coarse, work down one step at a time, and do not jump two grits in one pass.

**What is the difference between PSA, mesh and hook-and-loop 6 inch formats?** Paper PSA is the fast, cheap heavy-removal format. Mesh is the flexible, open weave that throws fine dust off the face, suited to wood and plaster. Hook-and-loop is the resandable format that stretches one disc across the whole grit sequence. Pick the format to the dust load and the number of grits on the job.

**What does the hole pattern matter?** The holes are the dust route under the pad. Fewer, larger holes pull more dust through; many small holes keep the disc stronger. Match the pattern to the pad you already run, and confirm the match before you buy.

**Can I use the same 6 inch disc on wood and on metal?** Yes, with a habit: clean the pad between materials, or run a dedicated disc for metal, because embedded grit from one material digs into the next. The grit sequence is the same either way.

**When should I use a belt instead of a 6 inch disc?** When the area is long, flat and open, and the job is continuous. The belt removes more material per minute at the same grit on a flat surface; the disc is the better tool on curves, corners and spot work. Many big-panel jobs use the belt for the long lines and the disc for the edges.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name the grit standard — FEPA or ISO 848 — plus the grit, the format, the diameter and the hole pattern. That keeps your order from being mixed with a different grit scale. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**How do I buy 6 inch sanding discs in bulk?** Send the grit, the format, the diameter, the hole pattern and the standard to a supplier, and build the conversation around cost per finished surface, not cost per disc. ### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum speed is at or below the disc's rated speed before every use, and keep the tool below it where the rating is not printed.
- Check the pad diameter and the hole pattern match the disc, and that a hook-and-loop disc sits fully on the pad, not just on its edge.
- Fit the guard where the tool carries one, and keep it on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc failure.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use extraction at the pad plus the right respiratory protection for the dust load.
- Confirm the grit, the grain, the format, the diameter, the hole pattern and the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every order so you do not mix scales.
- Follow the maker's installation notes, and on thin or soft material check the part for heat and for scratch depth after the coarse pass.
- Keep a record of the PPE and the dust controls you rely on; U.S. requirements are a useful baseline at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and setup guidance is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing for any wholesale or private label run, and start with the [quotation form](/quote) and the [OEM & private label service page](/oem).
