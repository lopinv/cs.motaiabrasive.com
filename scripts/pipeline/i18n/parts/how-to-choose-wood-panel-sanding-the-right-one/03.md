### Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistake. These are the ones that show up most often on panel work, with the fix that actually moves the problem:

- **Skipping a grit step.** Going from P100 straight to P320 leaves P100 scratches under the coat; add the P180 or P220 in between and the surface clears. The skip is visible at a raking light, and the re-sand is the real cost of the shortcut.
- **Holding a fine grit still on MDF.** A P320 held in one spot on an MDF face packs, glazes and burns a patch on the panel; the open-cut grit and the moving disc are the fix, not a coarser grit and more pressure.
- **Tearing the veneer with a coarse grit.** A P80 on a veneered panel will cut through the face at a joint; start at P150 or finer, skim, and check for torn spots under a raking light before the coat goes on.
- **Rounding the edge on the line.** A belt that is off tracking or off tension throws the scratch to one edge and leaves the other round; the fix is the tracking and tension set-up, and the edge gets a hand pass one grit finer than the face before the coat.
- **PSA edge-peel on a heavy panel face.** A PSA disc under real pressure on a large face can peel at the edge and leave a scalloped mark; the fix is a mesh disc on the router for the big faces, or lighter passes with the PSA, and the family is at [hook-and-loop sanding discs](/en/products/hook-loop-sanding-discs) and [5-inch mesh sanding discs](/en/products/5-inch-mesh-sanding-disc).
- **Running the tool past the rated speed.** A disc below the tool speed has to be matched down, and the tool brought down with it, never the other way; confirm the rated speed against the model before the run, and the guard stays on either way.
- **Storing the belts and discs warm and damp.** A curled belt tracks badly and a swollen paper disc does not lay an even scratch; store the rolls in the original wrap, dry and away from heat, and check the first panel of the shift before it goes to the next stage.
- **Mixing grit scales across suppliers.** A P150 from one source and a P150 from another are not the same grain size when the two sources are on different scales, and a panel that alternates between them shows the step; keep the shop on one scale and name it in the enquiry, and the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

For any of these, [the sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) and [the abrasive belt range](/en/collections/abrasive-belts) are where the grit, coat and form to reach for are visible, and the [abrasives glossary](/en/glossary) keeps the terms straight so you and your team are diagnosing the same problem. The thread through all eight is the one that runs through this whole article: a panel that comes out wrong is a match problem, not a bad abrasive, and the fix is the grit, the coat, the form or the set-up, chosen before the job starts.


### FAQ

**What is a good wood panel sanding sequence for a painted MDF cabinet?** In many cases P80 or P100 to flatten, P180 to even, and P320 as the last grit on the raw face, with P320 wet through the primer and between the topcoats. Start coarse, work down, and do not skip the de-raise between coats.

**What is the difference between a PSA disc and a mesh disc?** A PSA disc sticks onto a loop pad on a palm sander and is the everyday edge and small-face disc; a mesh disc is a cut-resistant nonwoven cloth that suits the faster, dustier router work on large faces. The grit is the same on both; the form is the difference.

**Can a wide belt line finish a panel paint-ready?** It can, when the line carries a fine stage and the grits step one band at a time, and the hand tools pick up the edge and any radius the belt does not reach. A panel that leaves the line on a P320 to 400 pass is ready for primer or the first coat.

**Why does my abrasive load on MDF?** The sugary fine dust packs the open grain and seals the face; an open-cut grit, a moving disc and the extractor at the work are the fix, and a coarser grit that cuts clean beats a fine grit that sits still.

**What grit before a sprayed clear coat on a hardwood panel?** P320 as the last dry grit on most hardwoods, and P400 between the coats on the topcoat itself. The clear coat has no hiding power, so the grit step has to be finer than you would run on a painted panel.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, plus the grit, the form, the size and the coat, because a P150 on one scale is not always a P150 on another; the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form is where the fields are.

**Do you make sanding discs and belts under our own label?** Yes, the [OEM & private-label service page](/en/oem) is where the label, the packing and the spec sheet on the box are set, with grits 60 to 5000 on the disc side and the four belt sizes on the belt side; minimum order and lead time are confirmed with sales before the run.

**How many discs should a panel run carry on the shelf?** The grits the sequence needs, one size up and one size down on the middle grit, and a pad for each disc family, because a run stops for a grit the shelf does not have more often than it stops for a bad disc.


### Before You Buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist for wood panel sanding. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's no-load speed, or the line's belt speed, is at or below the disc or belt's rated maximum working speed before every run; confirm the rated speed against the model and keep the tool below it.
- Check the disc form and the tool interface: PSA or hook-and-loop on a loop pad, mesh on the router, and the belt matched to the sander's width and speed.
- Fit and keep the guard or belt cover on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc or belt failure.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction at the work plus the right respiratory protection for the dust load.
- Name the grit standard, FEPA or ISO 848, in every enquiry, with the grit, the form, the size and the coat, so the order does not mix scales.
- Check the inspection report and any certificates for the specific run, especially on a coated or branded spec.
- Store the belts in the original roll and the discs flat, dry and away from heat, and follow the maker's set-up notes on tension and tracking.
- Confirm lead time, minimum order and packing on any [bulk or private-label order](/en/oem), and start with the [quotation form](/en/quote).
- For the personal side of the set-up, the U.S. PPE baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the practical abrasive set-up reading is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
