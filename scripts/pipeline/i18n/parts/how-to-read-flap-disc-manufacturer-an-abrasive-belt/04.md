### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistakes. Here is what shows up most often on the floor, with the fix that actually moves the problem:

- **Loading and glazing** — the face packs with soft material or the grit lays flat and stops cutting. Step to a finer grit, change to a grain tougher than the workpiece, and keep the disc moving instead of pressing into it.
- **Skipping grits** — going from P60 straight to P400 leaves P60 scratches under the finish. Add the P120 and P240 steps and the surface clears.
- **Wrong arbor** — a disc that does not sit fully on the flange walks at speed. Buy the right hole or a rated adapter; do not file the hole larger.
- **Running the tool fast** — if the tool speed is past the disc's printed maximum, lower the speed or use the smaller diameter, and never run a disc past what the label states.
- **Edge peel on the flaps** — the outer flaps lift and fray on a disc that is being pressed harder than its backing holds. Drop the pressure or move to the stiffer backing for that job; on a belt, the same failure shows up at the leading edge and reads the same way.
- **Heat on thin or soft parts** — a coarse grit on thin sheet builds heat that warps the part. Drop a grit and keep the disc moving rather than pressing in.

The thread through all six: the disc is only as good as the grit, grain, backing, arbor and speed you matched it with before the job started, and most complaints are really mismatch complaints wearing a grit's name. ### FAQ

**What should I ask a flap disc manufacturer before an order?** The five fields: grain and grit on a named scale, backing, diameter and arbor, and the printed maximum working speed. If a quotation cannot answer the speed question for the model, that is a pause signal, not a footnote.

**What grit do I start with for general grinding?** In many cases P80 to P120 covers everyday grinding and deburring on steel, then step down to P240 or P400 for blending and finish. Start coarse, work down, and never jump two steps at a time.

**How do I choose between the backings?** Steel for heavy removal where the disc has to stay rigid under pressure, fiberglass where the tool runs fast and the disc would flex or overheat on plastic, and plastic for lighter, cooler finishing. Pick by how hard the job is and how fast the tool runs; the [fiberglass-backing flap disc](/flap/fiberglass) and [steel-backing flap disc](/flap/steel) pages show the diameters in each.

**What does a higher grit actually do to the surface?** It removes less and leaves a finer, shallower scratch. Every pass has to be finer than the one before it, or the coarser scratches survive under the finish and show through.

**Can I use a 180 mm disc on the same grinder as a 125 mm disc?** Only if the guard, the flange and the tool speed all suit the larger diameter, and if the tool speed stays below the disc's printed maximum. When in doubt, check the tool rating and the disc label before you fit it.

**Which grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848, named in every order, because a number on one scale does not always match the number on the other. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and it is a field on the quotation, not a footnote.

**Do I need different grits for weld grinding and for finishing?** In practice, yes: the weld work wants P60 to P120 on a rigid backing, and the finishing wants P240 to P400 and up to the 4000 band on the backing that suits the speed. Two grits, one job, in that order — and the [metal fabrication abrasive use notes](/applications/metal-fabrication) walk the full sequence.

**How do I buy in bulk, or with my own label?** Send grit, backing, diameter, arbor and the standard to the supplier; the [flap disc range](/collections/flap-discs) is the picking list, and [request a quotation](/quote) starts the conversation. For a branded run, the [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) covers labels, packing and minimums.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm the tool's maximum speed is below the disc's printed maximum working speed before every use; if the quotation does not print a speed, confirm the rated speed against the model first.
- Check the arbor hole and flange are the correct size, and use a rated adapter where needed — never an enlarged hole.
- Fit and keep the tool guard on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc failure.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction with the right respiratory protection for the dust load.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) plus grit, grain, backing, diameter and arbor in every order so two suppliers cannot ship two discs for one number.
- Ask for the inspection report or the spec sheet behind the quotation, and confirm it against the label you received.
- Store discs dry and out of the heat, and keep them in the packaging until use; the [manufacturing and quality page](/factory) has the storage notes we follow.
- Keep any [flap disc range](/collections/flap-discs) or [abrasive belt](/collections/abrasive-belts) order on the same grit scale across the shop, and start branded or bulk runs with the [quotation form](/quote).
