### FAQ

**What grit range do 6 inch sanding discs cover?** From 60 to 5000, on the formats we supply — PSA, hook-and-loop, non-hole and mesh. The range is continuous, so a full ladder from stock removal to final polish can be bought on one standard.

**PSA or hook-and-loop: which should I stock?** Stock the one your grit-change rate matches. A shop that runs one grit at a time for long stretches is on PSA, because the cheaper disc carries the run. A shop that mixes grits on the same part or the same day is on hook-and-loop, because the plate carries the change and the waste per job drops. Many shops run both, and that is the honest answer.

**Can I use a 6-inch disc on a 4.5-inch or 5-inch tool?** In many cases yes, with the right adapter, and the check is the fit: the adapter must be rated for the tool and the disc, the balance must be true at no load, and the tool's speed must stay below the disc's rated speed. Confirm the rating against the model before the first pass, and if the combination feels off at no load, it will feel worse on the material.

**What grit do I run through filler on a panel?** In most panel work, P180 to P220 through the filler, then P320 to P400 before the topcoat, with P120 under the filler where the scratch is deep. The P180–P400 band is where the 220 rung lives, and that rung is the one a thin topcoat forgives the least. The depth of the filler is what moves the starting rung, so check that first.

**What grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848, and name the diameter, the format and the grain with it. The reference for the scale is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and naming the scale is what keeps two suppliers from quietly quoting two different discs for the same number: P220 on the two common scales is not the same cut, and the wrong one will show through a thin topcoat.

**Do 6-inch discs work on metal, or is that a flap disc job?** Both, at different rungs. A 6-inch disc at P100 to P400 is a strong option for blending and prep on steel and stainless; a heavy weld cap or a casting face is a flap disc or a belt job, and the [flap disc range](/en/collections/flap-discs) is where the heavy step lives. The split is removal versus finish, not metal versus wood.

**What is the minimum order, and how fast does it ship?** Confirm the minimum order quantity with sales at the abrasive factory, because it is set by the run, not the desk. In-stock grits in the standard range ship fast; a private-label or off-grid spec takes the label and the run time on top, and the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form is where the number comes back.

**How do I keep the grits I buy comparable across suppliers?** Keep the shop on one grit scale — FEPA or ISO 848 — and ask each supplier which scale their stock is on before the order, because a number on one scale does not match the number on another, and the mixed shelf is where the wrong cut arrives.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use — correct speed, correct fit, the right PPE, dust handled — the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm the tool's no-load speed is below the disc's or belt's rated speed before every use; if the rating is not on the part, confirm the rated speed against the model with the supplier.
- Check the plate, adapter, arbor and guard fit the disc or belt you are running, and that the combination is rated for it; a mismatch is the failure, not the part.
- Fit and keep the tool guard on at all times, and run the tool at no load with the operator clear before the first pass.
- Wear eye and face protection for the job, and use dust extraction at the tool with the respiratory class that matches the material, not the habit; the U.S. baseline is [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) and the setup guidance is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grit, the format, the grain and the diameter in every order, so the two ends of the transaction are quoting the same part.
- Ask for the inspection report or the lot check on any batch you are putting into production, and keep the packing list against it; the report is what the [manufacturing & quality page](/en/factory) describes as coming off the line.
- Store discs and belts cool and dry, away from the adhesive faces picking up dust, and keep the transport carton intact until the part is on the bench.
- Follow the install and speed notes that ship with the format you bought, and re-check the guard and the fit at the start of each shift, not just of the order.
- On any [OEM & private-label order](/en/oem) or a bulk [sanding disc range](/en/collections/sanding-discs) order, confirm the minimum order quantity, the lead time and the packing with sales before the run starts, using the [request a quotation](/en/quote) form.
