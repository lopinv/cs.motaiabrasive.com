### Q8: what to send in the enquiry, and the fields that make it

The fields are the order, and the supplier who answers all of them is the one to standardize on. The six, in the order the supplier reads them:

- **Grit and the standard** — the grit, and the FEPA or ISO 848 scale on the same one the shop buys to.
- **Grain and the charge** — the grain, and the charge when comparing two discs that both say the same grit.
- **Form and the face** — the paper, the open-web or the hole form, and the dry or water-resistant face, chosen to the tool and the process as above.
- **Quantity and lead time** — the order quantity and the shelf date, which sets whether it ships from stock or gets made to the run.
- **Label and packing** — the logo, the part code and the spec sheet on the box for a branded run.
- **The finish line** — the spec the sequence ends at, because the grit the next trade wants is the grit the order builds to.

Send those fields and the order is a conversation instead of a gamble. The [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) walks the branded run, and the [quotation form](/quote) takes the fields so the order starts with the ones that make or break it. One note on the numbers we share: we list the grit, the form, the grain and the standard as standard, and we confirm the rated speed against each model before an order ships, because that number has to come from the disc, not from a guess. The six fields are the Sandpaper Wholesale order, and the supplier who builds the disc to all six is the one the line standardizes on, because the one who builds to the price is the one the line outgrows by the second run.


### Speed, dust and the safety side of the shelf

The tool speed is the hard limit, and the disc has a rated maximum that the tool's no-load RPM has to sit under, and where the number is not printed, confirm the rating against the model and keep the tool below it. The orbital is the comfortable side of that line, and the angle grinder is the one where the check matters most, because its spindle speed is past the rating of the paper family, and the hole form on the holder is the compromise, with the speed check before the first pass.

Dust is the half that shows up in the health file 🛡️, and the fine sanding dust is easy to breathe in, with its own hazard on wood and on older finishes. The PPE baseline is at [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132, PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the wheel side of the read, where the guard and the speed and the dust sit together, is at [CCOHS, Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels). Wear eye and face protection on every pass, match the respiratory protection to the dust load when the extraction is not at the face, and put the waste in a sealed bin. Under compliant speed, the correct fit and the right PPE, the risk is reduced, not zero, and that is the honest line to keep at the bench.

The storage side of the shelf is where a bulk order lives, and the discs do not like the heat or the damp: a warped face does not square the way the new one does, and the disc shows the fault before the box does. The standards behind the numbers are at [ISO standards](https://www.iso.org/), and the grit standard that keeps the sequence comparable across the bulk order is FEPA, or ISO 848, named on the order so the two suppliers do not ship the two different cuts. The shelf that holds the grits in one scale and the faces named to the process is the shelf that survives the audit, and the label on the bin is where the standard and the face get written, because the bin is what the next operator sees.


### A wholesale day on the floor: the order the checks come in

The eight questions do not get answered on paper; they get answered on the floor, and the order they come in is the one that keeps the shelf honest. The day runs the checks in the order the fault is cheapest to catch:

- **Before the order leaves the bench** — the grit, the form, the grain and the standard are named, and the face is picked to the process. This is the check that catches the spec fault before the bulk ships, and it is the one that takes a minute.
- **At the sample** — the candidate runs on the scrap sheet, and the cut holds to the last pass. Where the face goes dull on the sample, the grain or the charge is wrong, and the swap is the disc, not the bulk.
- **At the shelf** — the grits are in one scale, the forms are labeled to the tool, and the face is named to the process. This is where the two-scale mix and the form mix get caught before the operator grabs the wrong one.
- **At the finish line** — the surface is clean, open and even, and the grit is the one the next trade wants. Where the finish shows a scratch, the missed step is the one two bands earlier, and the fix is the re-sand at that grit, not the press on a finer one.

The day is where the Sandpaper Wholesale discipline shows up as a habit rather than a rule, and the habit is the one that survives a shop change: the check at the bench, the look at the sample, the label on the shelf, and the finish that ends at the trade's line. The [abrasives glossary](/glossary) keeps the terms straight when the shop writes the day up, and the four checks are the four that belong on the job sheet, because the sheet is what the next operator sees.
