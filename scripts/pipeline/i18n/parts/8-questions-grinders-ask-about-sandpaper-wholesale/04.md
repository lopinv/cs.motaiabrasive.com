### Common mistakes, with the fix that actually moves it

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistakes. Here is what shows up most often on the wholesale shelf:

- **Buying the removal band deep and the finish band thin** 📦 — the shelf runs out on the fine grits and the surface goes back for the re-sand it already paid for; the sequence buys in one order, and the finish band is where it earns its keep.
- **The scale mixing on the shelf** ⚖️ — two suppliers, two scales, and the 150 that is not the 150; the standard is named on the order and the shop holds one scale so the grits are comparable.
- **The dry face under the wet process** — the charge breaks down on the first soak and the disc falls apart; the water-resistant face is the pick, and the face follows the process.
- **The form on the wrong tool** — the paper on the grinder, or the hole form under the orbital; the form follows the tool, and the shelf label keeps the two apart.
- **Leaning into a glazing face** 🔥 — the face stops cutting and starts burning; the grain is too soft for the material or the tool is held in one spot, and the fix is the grain or the movement, not the pressure.
- **The bulk shipping before the sample runs** — the cheap disc on the box is the one that glazes on the job; the sample against the scrap sheet is the match test, and the bulk follows the sample.

When you are not sure which grit to start on, the [sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) shows the grit and the form to reach for, the [automotive repair notes](/applications/automotive) give the sequence by repair type, and the [abrasives glossary](/glossary) keeps the terms straight so you and your team are diagnosing the same problem. The thread through all six is that the shelf that behaves badly on the floor is almost always a match problem, not a bad disc; fix the grit, the grain, the form, the face or the speed and the cut comes back.


### FAQ

**What grits should I standardize the shelf on?** The ladder is 60 to 120 for the removal, 150 to 180 for the leveling, 240 for the prep and 320 to 400 for the finish, and the sequence buys in one order, not grit by grit. The P2000 band is the fine end for the polished finish, and the rule is that the grit follows the material, the tool and the finish line.

**What is 2000 grit for on the volume line?** The P2000 band is the fine end, where the scratch goes light enough that the next coat sits clean over it, and the 1500 to P2000 range is where the polished finish lives. It is not a stripping grit, and the band is bought for the finish line, not the stripping end.

**Which grit standard do I name on the wholesale order?** FEPA, or ISO 848, written on the order so two suppliers do not quietly ship two different grits. It is a field on the spec sheet, not a footnote, and the reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), with the standards body at [ISO standards](https://www.iso.org/).

**Aluminum Oxide or the heat-tough grain on the stripping work?** The heat-tough grain, in many cases: it holds its bite on the long, hot pass and the face stays open where the general grain would pack. Aluminum Oxide is the everyday grain for the finishing bands, and the pick is the grain first and the grit second, because the charge follows the grain.

**When do I reach for the water-resistant face?** Where the process puts the face under water, and the wet sanding and the wet polish are the jobs. The dry face is the standard setup for the bench, and the water-resistant face is the one that survives the water, because the charge has to be the one that does not break down on the first soak.

**What is the cost number to manage on the order?** Cost per surface, not price per disc. The match test is the scrap sheet: run the candidate through the sequence, count the discs, and divide by the surfaces finished. The ratio is the number that belongs in the order conversation, and the bulk follows the sample.

**What fields do I send to a Sandpaper Wholesale supplier?** The grit and the standard, the grain and the charge, the form and the face, the quantity and the lead time, the label for a branded run, and the finish line the sequence ends at. The [quotation form](/quote) takes them all in one place, and the supplier who answers all of them is the one to standardize on.

**Do you do private label or wholesale on the discs?** The [OEM and private-label service page](/oem) covers the label, the packing and the spec sheet for a branded or retail run, and the [quotation form](/quote) takes the grit, the form, the grain and the quantity so the order starts with the fields that make or break it. For a first run, ask for a sample against the spec before the bulk ships.


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and under compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm your tool's maximum no-load speed sits below the disc's rated maximum before every use, and where the number is not printed, confirm the rating against the model.
- Check the form matches the tool: the paper form on the orbital, the open-web form on the dust-heavy job, and the hole form on the grinder holder, with the pairing on the shelf label.
- Fit and keep the tool guard on at all times; it is the first defence against a disc that releases its face.
- Wear eye and face protection on every pass, run dust extraction with the right filter for the material, and match the respiratory protection to the dust load when the extraction is not at the face.
- Name the grit standard — FEPA or ISO 848 — and the face — dry or water-resistant — in every enquiry, so the order does not mix scales or faces.
- Confirm the grain against the material: the general grain for the everyday work, the heat-tough grain for the demanding passes, and the slick-surface grain where the face would burn.
- Store the discs flat, away from heat and damp; a warped face does not hold the way the new one does, and the disc shows the fault before the box does.
- Run the sample against the scrap sheet before the bulk ships, because the two that survive the sheet are the two the bulk earns.
- For a wholesale or [private label](/oem) run, confirm the minimum order, the sequence and the lead time, and start the order at the [quotation form](/quote).
