### Q5: which form matches the tool, and what the shelf keeps

The form is where the tool decides, and the shelf that mixes forms by habit is the shelf that pays for it in disc time. The three forms the line runs on are:

- **The paper form on the orbital** — the everyday setup for the wood, the primed surface and the panel, and the grit that swaps in seconds. The form is the workhorse of the finishing world, and the [hook-and-loop sanding discs](/products/hook-loop-sanding-discs) page shows the sizes the orbital takes.
- **The open-web form for the dust-heavy job** — the slots keep the face working where the dust is the problem, and the form is the one for the enclosed work and the shop without good extraction.
- **The hole form on the grinder holder** — the compromise that takes the aggressive work at a size that fits the holder, and the form the real stripping belongs on rather than the paper.

The rule is that the form follows the tool and the grit follows both, and the shelf that mixes the three by habit is the shelf where the paper ends up on the grinder and the hole form ends up under the orbital. The [5-inch 8-hole hook-and-loop disc](/disc/gold8) is the grinder side of the 5-inch size, and the split between the two is the one to make once and keep on the shelf label, because the mix is the fault that shows as a disc that will not hold or a speed that will not clear. The Abrasive Paper sheet is the form for the wet polish and the fine work, and the sheet on the block is where the P2000 and finer grits live, because the cloth face is the one that survives the water and the pass, and the [sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) is where the cut and the sheet sides of the fine grit sit together.


### Q6: dry or waterproof, and where the face follows the process 💧

The face is where the process decides, and the call is the one that shows up on the surface when the face does not match the job.

- **The dry face** is the standard setup for the bench work, where the dust leaves through the tool and the face stays as built. It is the face for the panel line and the door, and the one the sequence above runs on.
- **The waterproof face** is the one that survives the water, and the charge is the field that keeps it working where the process puts the face under water. The charge has to be the one that does not break down in the water, and the disc is the one that keeps its cut through the wet pass instead of falling apart on the first soak.

The trap is the dry face under the wet process, and the fix is the face field on the order, because the charge that was built for the dry job is not the charge that survives the water. The rule is that the face follows the process, and the question on the enquiry is the one that names it: where the job is the wet sanding and the wet polish, the water-resistant face is the pick, and where it is the dry bench, the dry face is the one. The [quotation form](/quote) is where the face gets named, because the two faces are the same grit on the box until the process says otherwise. A water-resistant face on the shelf that the shop never runs wet is the one that cost the price of the water charge for a job that did not need it, and the face is the field to name before the bulk, because the two faces do not swap.


### Q7: what is the cost number, and how the sample earns the bulk

The cost number on a wholesale order is cost per surface, not price per disc, and the sample is where the two get separated before the bulk ships.

- **The disc that holds its cut** — the grain and the charge that run to the last pass without the face dulling, and the unit price is higher but the discs-per-surface is lower, so the ratio is the number that wins on the long run.
- **The disc that glazes** — the face that dulls by the second pass and the swap that is two a panel instead of one, and the unit price is lower but the discs-per-surface is higher, so the ratio is the number that loses.

The match test is the scrap sheet: take a piece of the job material, run the candidate disc through the sequence, count the discs, and divide by the surfaces finished. Do it against the incumbent and the two come back as one ratio, and that ratio is the number that belongs in the order conversation, not the per-disc price. The bulk follows the sample, and the sample runs on the job material, because the comparison that matters is the surface after, not the box in hand. One habit from the floor: keep the scrap sheet and the pass count the same for every candidate, and the [quotation form](/quote) is where the number gets built, because the two that survive the scrap sheet are the two the bulk earns. A Sandpaper Wholesale run that skips the sample is the run that ships the cheap disc into the line, and the fault shows up on the surface a week after the shelf is full, and the fix is the sample that was not run.
