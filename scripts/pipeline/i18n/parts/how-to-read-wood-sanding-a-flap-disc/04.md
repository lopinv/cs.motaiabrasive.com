### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection, the risk of the tool is reduced, not zero.

- Confirm the tool's no load speed is below the disc's printed maximum speed before every use, and re check it whenever the diameter or the tool changes.
- Check the diameter, the arbor hole and the flange match the tool and the guard, and fit a rated adapter where the hole differs — never file a hole larger.
- Keep the guard and flange fitted every pass; they are the first defence against a disc that fails at speed.
- Wear eye and face protection and the rated respiratory protection for the dust load, on every operator at the bench.
- Run dust extraction at the source, sized to what is coming off the face — stripping a coated face is a heavier load than raw timber, so match them separately.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848), the grit, the backing, the diameter, the arbor and the grain in every order, so a two supplier shelf does not mix two scales under one number.
- Ask for the inspection or test report that backs the rated speed figure on the disc you are buying, and keep it with the tool file.
- Store discs flat, dry and out of direct heat, oldest stock to the tool first, and follow the maker's install notes for the flange and the guard before the first pass.
- For a bulk or branded order, confirm the minimum order, the lead time and the label, and start the conversation with the [request a quotation](/quote) form, the sequences by wood at [woodworking sanding use](/applications/woodworking), and the full set at [the flap disc range](/collections/flap-discs).
