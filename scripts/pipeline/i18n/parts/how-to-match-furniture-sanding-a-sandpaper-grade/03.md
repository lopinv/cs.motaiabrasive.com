### Cost, life and lead time

The cost question on furniture sanding is not the price per disc, it is the cost per surface finished. A cheaper PSA disc that changes more often can cost more per panel than a pricier mesh disc that carries further over the same work, which is why the number to ask a supplier to help work out is the cost per finished panel, not the sticker price.

Where a line moves from discs to belts, the economics flip. A casegoods line that sands thousands of edges a week usually runs a belt sander for the heavy pass, and [the Y399 abrasive belt](/model/y399) and [the 610 × 100 mm abrasive belt](/products/610x100-abrasive-belt) cover the smaller belt sanders, with the wider 1520 × 630 and 2200 × 1330 loops carrying the continuous sanders on door and casegoods lines. The full set is in [the abrasive belt range](/collections/abrasive-belts).

Wear rate is the number you will not find in a catalogue, and it is not a number we invent: it moves with the material, the machine, the pressure and the grit. The honest answer on any order is to run one panel and let that panel count, or send the material and the tool and let the run be sized against that. On bulk or branded orders the extra work is the label, the packing and the spec sheet, and the MOQ and lead time get confirmed with sales before the run is planned. [The OEM & private label service page](/oem) explains what can go on the packaging, and [request a quotation](/quote) is where the conversation starts.

The fields that make or break that conversation:

- Grit and grain, stated on the same scale the shop runs.
- Size and backing, matched to the tool and mandrel.
- Format: PSA, hook-and-loop, mesh, solid, or belt.
- The standard, FEPA or ISO 848, written on the line.
- Quantity, packing, and the label work on any branded run.
- Rated speed confirmation against the model, so the number comes from the product, not a guess.


### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The fixes are usually cheaper than the mistakes. What turns up most on furniture lines, with the fix that actually moves the problem:

- **Skipping a grit step.** P80 straight to P400 leaves the P80 scratches under the coat. What it looks like: a finish that is rough in places that should be smooth. Add the middle grits and the surface clears.
- **Sanding in circles.** Swirl marks under a stain or clear coat. What it looks like: concentric rings in raking light. Sand in one direction, with the grain, and the finish lands even.
- **Holding the tool still.** A dug-out groove and a burnt patch. What it looks like: a dish in the panel that no finish will hide. Keep the tool moving and let the grit do the removal.
- **Glazing on sticky wood.** The face stops cutting on a resinous board or on MDF. What it looks like: the cut slows and the face goes shiny. A harder bond, an open-structure grain, or a mesh disc fixes it, and pressing harder makes it worse.
- **The wrong backing for the tool.** PSA on a tool that wants hook-and-loop, or the reverse. What it looks like: discs shifting and edges scalloping. Match the disc to the mandrel or pad it actually runs on.
- **Too fast for a fine grit.** A P400 on a maxed-out orbital glazes and warms the panel. What it looks like: a shiny, uneven fine-sanded face. Drop the tool speed or step the grit down, and keep the disc moving.
- **Edges and face out of step.** An edge sanded coarser than the face, or the reverse. What it looks like: the panel reads two-tone in raking light. Keep the edge grit equal to, or one step finer than, the face grit.
- **Storage that kills the bond.** Discs kept in a warm, damp corner lose bond and grit. What it looks like: new discs that cut like old ones. Keep the stock flat, dry, and off the floor.

The thread through all eight is that the abrasive is as good as the grit, backing, grain and speed that got matched to it before the panel. Most of the complaints are mismatches wearing a grit's name. For the grit and backing to reach for on any of these, [the sanding disc range](/collections/sanding-discs) and [the abrasives glossary](/glossary) keep the terms straight so the whole team is diagnosing the same problem.


### FAQ

**What grit should I start furniture sanding on?** P80 to P120 when taking down an old coat or a scarred surface, and P120 to P150 when the board is already clean and heading for stain or oil. Start at the coarsest grit that handles what is underneath, and step up from there.

**What is the difference between P240 and P320?** P240 is the workhorse block grit for matte finishes and most stains, and P320 closes the surface finer for the hard finishes and anything that will reflect light. The step between them is one, which is why the two are so often the last two stops on a sequence.

**PSA or hook-and-loop on a random orbital?** PSA is cheaper per disc and cuts light and even; hook-and-loop suits the bench that swaps grits constantly, because the mandrel stays on the tool and the disc pops off clean. Either runs on a standard orbital, and matching the disc to the pad keeps the swap fast either way.

**What grit for stained wood?** A 240 grit finish in one direction with the grain is the workhorse; where the stain is very thin or the figure is open, the run carries to P320 so the stain does not show the scratch under it.

**What standard should I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848 grit, plus grain, size and backing, on the line of the enquiry. That keeps the order off a different scale and keeps two suppliers from shipping two different grits against the same number. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard (ISO 848)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA).

**How many discs should I plan for a panel?** The number moves with the material, the machine and the pressure, so no catalogue number is honest. Run one panel and let it count, or send the material and the tool and the number gets sized against that before the rest of the run is ordered.

**Can you put my brand on the discs or belts?** Yes, that is the OEM and private label side: the label, the packing and the part code on the grits the shop runs. The MOQ and lead time get confirmed with sales, and the starting point is [the OEM & private label service page](/oem).
