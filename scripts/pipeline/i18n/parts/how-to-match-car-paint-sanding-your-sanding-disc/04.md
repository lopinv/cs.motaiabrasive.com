### Common mistakes and how to fix them

The fixes here are usually cheaper than the mistakes. Here is what shows up most often on panel work, with the fix that actually moves the problem:

- **Skipping grits.** Going from P80 straight to P400 leaves P80 scratches under the finish; add the P180 and P320 steps in between and the surface clears.
- **Starting too fine on a coarse surface.** A P320 disc over heavy filler or old paint glazes and loads instead of cutting. Step back to P120 or P180 and work up, rather than forcing a fine disc into a coarse job.
- **Edge-wrap on a hook-and-loop disc.** The disc edge catches a panel corner and walks. Keep the pressure off the edge as you near it, and re-seat the disc flat on the mandrel before the next pass.
- **Speed mismatch.** Running the tool above the disc's rated maximum. Confirm the rating against the model, lower the tool speed, or move to a smaller disc, and keep the disc inside its printed maximum.
- **Dust carryover.** Coarse grit dust tracked into a fine pass scrates the panel with embedded grit. Clean the panel between grits, run extraction at the work, and do the fine pass last, when the shop is at its cleanest.
- **Wrong grain for the material.** Zirconia on a filler job cuts too aggressively and chases the surface. Aluminium oxide is the default for paint and filler work; silicon carbide is the pick for the slicker, non-ferrous panels. Match the grain to the material, then set the grit to the cut.

The thread that runs through all of them is that a bad panel finish is almost always a match problem, not a bad disc. ### FAQ

**Which grit do I start with on a bare panel?** In many cases P80 to P120 to level, then P180 to even out, then P320 to P400 before primer or base coat. Start coarse, work down, and never jump two steps at a time.

**Why P320 for the finish pass?** P320 is the grit band most shops treat as the standard finish over primer and filler, because base coat is designed to cover that scratch line. If your top layer is thin, P400 gives a softer scratch instead.

**What is the difference between a hook and loop disc and a solid paper disc?** The hook-and-loop disc snaps to a mandrel and can be swapped between grits, which keeps one tool for the whole sequence. The solid paper disc is single use, cheaper to run, and fits the mandrel arbor directly. Pick the attachment that keeps the disc clean and flat for your sequence.

**When do I use a mesh disc instead of a round disc?** For large, flat areas and filler-heavy work, where the open face keeps material from packing the disc and the cut stays even across the panel. The 5 inch and 6 inch mesh sizes cover most car and truck panels.

**The disc will not stay stuck to the tool face.** Check the adhesive layer on both sides. If the mandrel face is flattened or clogged with dust, swap in a clean mandrel, and if the disc's own adhesive is spent, move to a fresh disc rather than forcing it on.

**How do I buy a bulk or private-label run?** Send the grit on the FEPA or ISO 848 scale, the diameter, the attachment, the grain, and the standard name to a supplier. A P320 batch can cover a whole fleet, and the [request a quotation](/quote) page is where that order starts. **What standard do I name in an enquiry?** FEPA or ISO 848 grit, plus the diameter, the attachment and the grain. The reference is at [the FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA), and keeping the shop to one scale keeps the grits on the shelf comparable.

**What is the safety baseline for these tools?** Eye and face protection, respiratory protection for the dust load, extraction running at the work, and the disc kept inside its rated speed. The U.S. PPE baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the wheel setup is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).


### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these tools is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm the tool's rated speed against the model and keep every disc below its printed maximum working speed for each use.
- Confirm the mandrel arbor and the disc backing match, and that the guard or tool setup is in place for the disc size.
- Wear eye and face protection, and run dust extraction with respiratory protection sized to the dust load before the fine grits.
- Confirm grit, attachment, diameter, grain and the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every order, and land the 320 finish before base coat goes on.
- Seat the hook and loop disc flat on the mandrel before every pass, so the edge cannot lift and wrap at a panel corner.
- Check storage and transport for any bulk order: grit and attachment that arrive crushed or contaminated lose their cut, so confirm how the supplier packs and ships.
- Follow the maker's install notes for the attachment, and check the panel and the disc for heat and scratch depth on thin or soft material.
- Keep a record of the PPE and extraction controls you rely on; the U.S. baseline is at [OSHA PPE general requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132), and the wheel setup is at [CCOHS: Abrasive Wheels](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/abrasive_wheels).
