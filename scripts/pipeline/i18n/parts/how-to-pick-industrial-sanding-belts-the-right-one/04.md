### Wide belt systems: the production decision

When the job is a surface and not a part, the wide belt sander is the tool, and the grit decision stretches across the whole line instead of one operation. The 2200 × 1330 mm wide belt for wide belt sanders is the production grade, and the sequence on a typical line is P40 or P60 to open, P80 to P120 to even, P320 to P400 to clean, and P1000 to P2000 for a fine pre-coat. Two things that differ from bench work and that decide the whole setup: dust control at the belt entry and exit, and edge wear management over a long shift. A wide belt line that runs eight hours will show edge lift by the afternoon if the machine is not set right, and that is the first sign the belt is due for a change or the contact wheel needs a re-set. The grit standard you name on the wide belt order should be the same one you name on the bench — FEPA or ISO 848 — so the grits on the shelf are comparable across the whole shop. ### FAQ

**What grit do I start with on most industrial jobs?** In many cases P120 covers the everyday band for steel and general work, then step down to P240 or P400 for blending and pre-coat. Start coarse, work finer, and never jump two steps — a P60 scratch under a P400 coat is the classic visible failure.

**What grit standard should I name in an enquiry?** Name FEPA or ISO 848 in every order so the grit on the belt matches the grit on the next belt. A P120 on one scale is not always a P120 on another, and the [FEPA grit standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEPA) reference is the one to keep open when the label is new.

**How do I match my machine RPM to the belt?** Confirm the machine no-load speed, work out the loop line speed for your belt length, and keep that number at or below the belt's printed maximum. The loop length and the belt rating both come off the spec sheet — ask for both in one reply and you avoid a wrong-speed order.

**Why does my belt stop cutting?** Usually loading and glazing, or a grit that is too fine for the material, or a grain that packs on the workpiece. Step the grit, change the grain, and keep the belt moving. If the face looks fine and the cut is still down, check the edge for lift and the machine setup for tension.

**What does a loop belt do that a flat belt does not?** A loop belt wears in stages because the abrasive is folded back on itself, so it keeps cutting longer and the machine runs longer between changes. That is why most industrial stock is loop construction — the life and the evenness of the cut over a shift is the whole point.

**Do I need a P2000 grit, or is P400 enough?** P400 does the real pre-coat cleanup; P2000 is the fine stage that de-raises the P320 and P400 marks on a surface that is already close. On a clear coat or a fine topcoat, P2000 is the grit that softens the last machine marks and the surface takes the finish clean. Ask your supplier if P2000 is on the standard run for your width.

**How do I pick an abrasive belt supplier for a bulk order?** Send the grit, the grain, the width, the quantity and the grit standard in one enquiry and ask for the loop length and the maximum line speed against your machine. ### Before you buy

A short compliance and purchasing checklist. This is a reminder, not a substitute for your own site rules, and in compliant use with the right protection the risk of these machines is reduced but not zero.

- Confirm your machine's no-load speed against the belt's rated maximum line speed, and keep the tool at or below it.
- Check the platen or arbor and the guards are the correct fit for the belt width, and that the loop sits fully on the contact wheel.
- Wear eye and face protection, and use dust extraction and the right respiratory protection for the dust load — wood fines, metal fines, silica where present.
- Run dust extraction at the point of generation, with the right filter for the material, and check the line every shift.
- Name the grit standard (FEPA or ISO 848) in every enquiry so the grit on the belt matches the grit on the next belt.
- Check the inspection report and the certificates for the model you are ordering, and keep a copy with the job record.
- Plan storage and transport for long wide belts: keep them flat, dry and away from heat, and check the loop for edge lift before the first run.
- Follow the belt maker install notes, and re-check the contact wheel tension and the edge trimmer after every belt change.
- Confirm the grit, the grain, the width, the backing, the quantity and the lead time on any [abrasive belt range](/collections/abrasive-belts) or [wide belt](/products/2200x1330-abrasive-belt) order, and start with the [quotation form](/quote).
