The short answer: why a mesh face keeps cutting

The one rule that governs the choice: pick the open web when the dust is the enemy, and keep the solid face when it is not. A Mesh Sanding Discs face is a web of cut slots instead of a solid paper disc, and the slots are the whole argument. The dust passes through the face instead of sitting on it, and the cut stays even through the pass instead of loading and going brown. That is the difference between the mesh and the plain paper on the dirty job, and it is the reason the mesh earns a premium on the job that throws dust and nothing on the job that does not.

The eight questions below come through on real grinder calls, in the order the purchase decision happens: what the web buys you, the grit sequence on it, the dust and speed checks, and then cost, life and lead time. The sanding disc range is the catalogue behind the eight, and it is where a buyer checks which grits of the mesh ship from stock before the order goes out.

In one line: the mesh is not a finer disc, it is a cleaner one, and the clean face is where the finished panel comes from.

  • 🕸️ The open web — the cut slots let the dust through the face, and the face stays clean through the pass instead of packing.
  • The grit range — the same grits as the paper, and the range we supply runs P60 to P5000, and the P5000 end is where the wet and the polish work lives.
  • The job fit — the mesh is the pick on the enclosed work and the work with dust extraction, and the plain paper is the one for the clean, dry job.
